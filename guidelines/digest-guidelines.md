# Daily Digest Guidelines

## Philosophy

The digest exists so the reader can keep a pulse on the world in under 5 minutes. Simplicity above all. Good diagrams land data instantly, but only when the data calls for it. Words carry the story. Diagrams support the words when numbers or comparisons are involved.

## Digest page (the daily file)

- One HTML file per day. Named `YYYY-MM-DD.html`.
- Raw HTML. Zero CSS. Default browser fonts, colors, link styles.
- H1: the date. One sentence intro. Nothing else before the first story.
- Each story: H2 headline, 1-2 short paragraphs, then `<p><a href="...">more</a></p>`.
- Keep it dense. Not too many headers, not too many lines. A scan, not an article.
- "Everything else" section at the bottom: a `<ul>` of one-liners.
- Previous/next day links near the top. No "next day" link if this is the latest digest.
- Disclaimer at the bottom: sources listed, "I'm a language model" note.
- No em dashes. Periods and commas.

## "More" pages (deeper pages)

- One page per story. Linked from the digest.
- "back to digest" link at top and bottom.
- More detail than the digest summary. Include the key facts: who, what, where, when, why, and what happens next. Cover the background that makes the story make sense. Aim for 4-6 paragraphs of substance across 2-3 sections. Not a long article, but enough that the reader walks away informed without needing the source.
- One external source link at the bottom so the reader can go deeper if they choose. Just one. Not a list.
- Cross-link to related internal pages when the connection is real.
- Every "more" page must have at least one rough.js diagram. Find the angle: a comparison, a trend, a timeline, a composition. There is always something worth charting if you look for it.
- But the diagram must earn its place. It should show something that words alone would struggle to convey. A diagram that just restates the text is clutter.
- Diagrams must be tasteful. Never visualize human deaths as counted objects (squares, dots, icons). The diagram should illuminate the story, not sensationalize it.

## Linking

- Days link to each other (previous/next).
- Deeper pages link back to their digest.
- Deeper pages cross-link when stories relate (e.g., midterms links to nvidia-selloff).

## Editorial

- Describe what happened. Don't editorialize.
- Note patterns when they're obviously there. Don't force them.
- Mention historic context when it grounds the story. Don't speculate about meaning.
- Short sentences. Direct. Say it once.
- Use **bold** for key phrases in lists and paragraphs. It makes the page scannable. The reader should be able to skim the bold text alone and get the story.
- Note when something is unconfirmed.
- Err on the side of simplicity. Always.
