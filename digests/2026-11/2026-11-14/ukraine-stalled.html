Ukraine says Russia refused to discuss ceasefire at Abu Dhabi talks

The talks collapse

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said Russia's delegation at the Abu Dhabi talks told Ukrainian negotiators it lacked the authority to discuss a ceasefire and had no instructions from Moscow to do so. The talks, which ran for three days, ended Wednesday with only a prisoner exchange deal to show for the effort. Ukraine had come to the table expecting a preliminary framework discussion; Russia appeared to treat the session primarily as a venue for prisoner logistics. Zelensky called the outcome "deeply disappointing" and said Ukraine would not return to the table without a clearer mandate from Moscow.

Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov accused European governments of using the pause in fighting to help Ukraine rebuild its military and resupply its stockpiles. He said Russia's position on territorial sovereignty had not changed and that any framework would have to begin from the current front line, not Ukraine's internationally recognized borders. The European Union rejected Lavrov's characterization and called for an immediate ceasefire as a precondition for further talks.

What comes next

U.S. envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff briefed European leaders after their separate meeting with Putin in Moscow on Thursday. Officials briefed on the conversation said Putin had indicated he would consider further talks but only if Ukraine agreed in advance to discuss territorial issues. The next round of talks has not been scheduled. Trump said on Friday that Ukraine may ultimately have to accept territorial changes as part of any realistic settlement, drawing protests from Kyiv and several European capitals.

The UN reported 10 civilians killed and 60 wounded in Ukraine in the two days after the talks ended, concentrated in Odesa and Zaporizhzhia. The humanitarian situation has remained severe throughout the negotiating period. Aid organizations said they were struggling to maintain access to communities near the contact line. The collapse of the Abu Dhabi framework leaves no active multilateral channel for the conflict for the first time since October.
