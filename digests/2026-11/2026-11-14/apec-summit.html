Trump to meet Xi and Putin at APEC. Nuclear arms and Iran on the agenda.

The summit announcement

The White House confirmed on Friday that President Trump will hold a trilateral meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin on November 19 in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. It will be the first three-way summit between the sitting leaders of the United States, Russia, and China. Senior officials in all three governments confirmed the meeting. No formal agreements are expected, with one senior administration official telling Reuters it is intended as the start of a conversation rather than a negotiation.

The announcement came as Trump completed a series of bilateral calls with both leaders over the past week. The White House said Trump proposed the trilateral format after separate conversations convinced him that a joint session could move faster than sequential diplomacy. Xi agreed on the condition that Taiwan not be raised. Putin agreed after U.S. envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff briefed him in Moscow on Thursday.

Nuclear arms

The primary U.S. goal for the meeting is to begin discussions on reducing nuclear stockpiles. The New START treaty between the U.S. and Russia expired in February 2026 without a successor agreement, leaving no bilateral limits on strategic warheads for the first time since 1972. Trump has argued that any new arms control framework must include China, whose nuclear arsenal has expanded significantly since 2020. Beijing has historically refused to participate in U.S.-Russia arms control talks, citing the disparity in stockpile sizes.

Iran and what is off the table

The second major agenda item is the U.S. conflict with Iran. Trump said he wants Russian and Chinese cooperation to pressure Tehran toward a deal. Both Moscow and Beijing have maintained economic ties with Iran throughout the conflict. A senior administration official said Trump would ask Putin and Xi to suspend certain energy purchases from Iran as a negotiating lever. No commitments are expected at this stage.

Russia's war in Ukraine will not formally appear on the agenda, the official said, in line with Putin's conditions for attending. European governments expressed unease with the format, with French President Macron saying it risked legitimizing Russian aggression. Ukraine was not invited and has not been consulted. Trump said Ukraine may have to accept territorial changes as part of any eventual settlement.
