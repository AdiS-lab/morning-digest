Stock market rebounds after three-day plunge

November 10, 2026

What happened

U.S. equity markets recovered Monday after three consecutive days of heavy losses, with the Nasdaq Composite and S&P 500 both closing up more than 1.5 percent as bargain hunters rotated back into beaten-down growth stocks. The bounce came despite inflation data showing core consumer prices rising 0.3 percent in October, above expectations, which kept pressure on Federal Reserve officials to maintain a cautious posture on interest rates.

The recovery was uneven. Nvidia fell 5.47 percent on Monday, extending its losses as the spy scandal surrounding CEO Jensen Huang continued to weigh on the stock. Shares have now lost nearly $400 billion in market capitalization over the past five trading days, one of the fastest destructions of equity value in U.S. stock market history. Other AI and semiconductor names, including AMD and Broadcom, did manage modest gains.

Background: the Nvidia spy scandal

The sell-off traces directly to revelations first reported on November 9 that CEO Jensen Huang admitted to a long-term affair with a woman whom U.S. counterintelligence officials believe was recruited by Chinese intelligence. Federal investigators are examining whether proprietary chip design information was accessed or communicated during the relationship. Huang has not been charged with any crime, and Nvidia's board said it had opened an internal investigation.

The company's stock had already been under pressure from the broader market downturn triggered by the midterm election results and the Iran crisis. The spy scandal added a corporate governance dimension that rattled institutional investors. Nvidia supplies the majority of the world's AI training chips, and any disruption to its leadership or business would reverberate across the technology sector.

What happens next

Analysts are divided on whether Monday's market bounce is a durable recovery or a brief relief rally. The core inflation reading of 0.3 percent suggests the Fed will keep rates elevated into the first quarter of 2027, limiting the tailwind that falling rates would otherwise provide for high-multiple tech stocks. Options markets imply continued high volatility in both Nvidia and the broader Nasdaq through year-end.

For Nvidia specifically, the board faces pressure to clarify Jensen Huang's status and the scope of the federal inquiry. Institutional shareholders including major pension funds have begun asking pointed questions about governance. A prolonged investigation, even without criminal charges, could impair the company's ability to close government contracts and obtain export licenses for its chips.
