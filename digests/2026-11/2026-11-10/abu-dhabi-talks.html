Russia and Ukraine hold talks in Abu Dhabi as U.S. mediates

November 10, 2026

What happened

Russian and Ukrainian delegations met for nearly four hours in Abu Dhabi on Monday, their first formal negotiation session since February 2026. U.S. Special Envoy Jared Kushner chaired the talks and told reporters afterward that conversations had "really identified, narrowed the gaps" between the two sides. Both delegations signaled flexibility on territorial arrangements, though no agreement was announced and significant obstacles remain on sovereignty, security guarantees, and the status of Russian-occupied regions.

The UAE brokered the venue, continuing its role as a neutral host for sensitive international negotiations. The talks come one day after Russia and Ukraine traded drone and missile strikes, including Ukrainian drone attacks on Russian oil refineries that have reduced Russia's refining capacity by 30 percent over the past week.

Background

The last formal negotiation session between the two countries had collapsed in February 2026 over disagreements on the size of a neutral buffer zone and Ukraine's demand for NATO-equivalent security guarantees. The front lines have remained largely static since mid-2025, with Russia controlling roughly 20 percent of Ukraine's pre-war territory. Ukraine's drone campaign against Russian oil infrastructure, which began in late October 2026, has been widely read as a pressure tactic ahead of these talks, forcing Moscow to come to the table with its energy exports visibly degraded.

The choice of Abu Dhabi reflects the UAE's cultivated neutrality. The Gulf state has maintained diplomatic ties with both Moscow and Kyiv throughout the war, and Crown Prince Sheikh Mohammed bin Zayed has spoken with both Putin and Zelensky in recent months. The United States, under Kushner's mediation, has pushed for a ceasefire framework that would freeze territorial lines roughly where they stand, a proposal Ukraine has accepted in principle while insisting on robust security guarantees.

What happens next

Both delegations are expected to report back to their governments before a follow-up session. The key sticking points are territorial sovereignty over the four Ukrainian oblasts Russia annexed in 2022, the size and mandate of any international peacekeeping force, and Ukraine's future relationship with NATO. Russia has demanded Ukraine formally renounce NATO membership as part of any deal; Ukraine and its Western backers have rejected that as a precondition.

Kushner indicated a third session could happen within two weeks if both sides submit written positions. European leaders, who were notably absent from Abu Dhabi, have called for inclusion in any formal negotiations, fearing a U.S.-Russia bilateral deal that could leave European security interests unaddressed.
