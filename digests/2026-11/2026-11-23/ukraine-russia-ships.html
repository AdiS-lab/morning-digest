Ukraine strikes Russian ship in the Baltic and a warship in a Libyan port.

A cargo ship in the Baltic

Ukraine struck a Russian-flagged cargo vessel in the Baltic Sea on Saturday in what Ukrainian military intelligence described as a long-range drone operation. The ship, which Ukraine said had been carrying military supplies to Kaliningrad, sustained damage to its stern and was towed to a Swedish anchorage, where the Swedish coastguard said it was not leaking fuel. Russian state media acknowledged an attack on a vessel in the Baltic but said the drone had been intercepted and damage was minor. Sweden said it was investigating the incident and was in contact with Ukraine.

The cargo vessel is the first Russian ship struck by Ukraine outside the Black Sea and the broader Eastern Mediterranean theater. Ukraine's military intelligence directorate released a short statement saying the ship had been identified as a regular supply run to Russian forces in Kaliningrad and had been tracked for several days before the strike. The operation required a drone flight of roughly 800 kilometers over open sea, well beyond Ukraine's previous demonstrated range for maritime strikes. NATO allies said privately they were concerned the Baltic operation had been conducted without advance coordination with Sweden or other Baltic littoral states.

A warship in Libya

A day later, on Sunday, Ukraine hit a Russian frigate moored at the port of Tobruk in eastern Libya. Satellite imagery analysts identified the vessel as the Grigorovich-class frigate Admiral Essen, which had been using Libyan ports controlled by the Haftar administration as resupply stops for operations in the Eastern Mediterranean. The strike damaged the ship's forward deck. Ukraine's military intelligence said the operation was coordinated with the Baltic strike as a demonstration that no Russian naval asset was out of range, regardless of where it was berthed.

Libya's internationally recognized government in Tripoli said it had not authorized Russian naval vessels to use Tobruk and demanded explanations from both Russia and Ukraine. Russia's defense ministry did not comment on the Libya strike. NATO allies privately expressed serious concern about the Libya operation's implications for Mediterranean security and the possibility of a Ukrainian strike inside a third country drawing other regional actors into the conflict. The EU's foreign policy chief said Brussels was seeking clarification from Kyiv before making a formal statement.

What the strikes mean

The two operations represent the most significant geographic expansion of Ukraine's campaign beyond its own territory and the Black Sea since the war began in 2022. Ukraine has previously struck Russian targets in Crimea, hit the Kerch Bridge three times this month, and conducted drone raids deep into Russian territory. Reaching the Baltic and the Mediterranean marks a new phase. Military analysts said the operations were likely designed to serve dual purposes: to degrade Russian logistics and demonstrate to Moscow that it cannot assume rear areas are safe, while also signaling to Western allies that Ukraine retains offensive capability despite Russian pressure on its front lines.

The strikes also complicate Ukraine's diplomatic position. Sweden, which only joined NATO in 2024, is now hosting a damaged Russian vessel in its waters and is under pressure to explain how Ukraine conducted such an operation through Swedish airspace or maritime exclusion zones without notification. The Libya strike raises similar questions about Ukrainian drones transiting Mediterranean airspace. Ukrainian officials said they would engage with any allied concerns through diplomatic channels but would not apologize for targeting a vessel actively supplying Russian military forces.
