Japan scrambles jets after Russian aircraft violate its airspace

The incident north of Hokkaido

Japan's Defense Ministry said two suspected Russian military aircraft violated Japanese airspace north of Hokkaido at approximately 6:40 a.m. local time on Tuesday. The aircraft flew roughly 20 kilometers into Japanese territorial airspace over waters between Hokkaido and the disputed Kuril Islands before turning back. Japan launched F-35A fighters from Chitose Air Base in response. The jets intercepted the aircraft at the airspace boundary after the intruding planes had already exited.

The Defense Ministry identified the aircraft as likely reconnaissance planes based on their flight profile, though it declined to name a specific model or confirm whether they were armed. No hostile actions were taken and no shots were fired. Japan's Chief Cabinet Secretary described the violation as "extremely regrettable" in a morning press briefing and said Tokyo had lodged a strong formal protest with Moscow through diplomatic channels. Russia's embassy in Tokyo had not responded publicly as of Tuesday evening.

Japan's diplomatic response

Defense Minister Minoru Kihara summoned Russia's ambassador to the Defense Ministry on Tuesday afternoon to deliver a formal protest. Kihara said the violation constituted a "clear breach of Japanese sovereignty" and demanded a full explanation of the aircraft's mission and authorization. The government said it would raise the matter at the United Nations if Russia failed to provide a satisfactory response within 48 hours.

Prime Minister Fumio Kishida convened an emergency meeting of the National Security Council on Tuesday morning. Officials briefed the U.S. Embassy in Tokyo and said Japan was seeking confirmation from Washington that the incident would be discussed with Moscow at the Shenzhen summit. The U.S. State Department said it was consulting with Japanese counterparts and took airspace violations "very seriously." Japan's government said it would continue monitoring Russian military activity near Hokkaido closely over the coming days.

Context and significance

Japan has tracked Russian military activity near its northern islands for decades, and the Japan Air Self-Defense Force has conducted several hundred scrambles per year against Russian aircraft in recent years. Most intrusions involve Russian planes flying near but not into Japanese airspace. Tuesday's violation is considered one of the most serious precisely because the aircraft entered sovereign airspace rather than simply approaching the boundary. Japan's Defense Ministry said it had no prior warning of the aircraft's approach through diplomatic channels, which it described as highly unusual.

The incident comes as the broader U.S.-Russia relationship is undergoing a visible shift ahead of the Shenzhen trilateral. Some Japanese analysts said Russia may have used the violation to test Japan's response capabilities at a moment when Washington's attention is focused on the summit. Japan and Russia have a long-standing territorial dispute over the four Kuril Islands, which Japan calls the Northern Territories. That dispute has prevented the two countries from signing a formal peace treaty since the end of World War II.
