Russia hits both Kyiv bridges. Ukraine strikes the Kerch Bridge.

The overnight missile strike on Kyiv

Russia fired seven ballistic missiles at Kyiv in the early hours of Tuesday, November 17. Five of the seven reached their targets. The North Bridge and the South Bridge, the two main crossings over the Dnipro River, were both struck and structurally damaged. Mayor Vitali Klitschko declared a city emergency shortly after 6 a.m., closing both bridges and suspending the Green Line metro, which uses both spans. Water supply was cut to the left-bank districts of Darnytsia and Bortnychi. Rolling blackouts began across four districts by mid-morning.

Ukrainian air defense units intercepted two of the seven incoming missiles before they reached the city. The remaining five caused the bridge strikes and hit a road interchange on the northern approach. Emergency engineering teams were on-site within two hours to assess structural integrity. Klitschko said both crossings would remain closed until full inspections were complete, with no timeline given for reopening.

Ukraine's response: striking the Kerch Bridge

Several hours after the Kyiv attacks, Ukraine struck the Kerch Bridge, the 19-kilometer span connecting Russia to occupied Crimea. Ukraine's military confirmed it used a combination of sea-launched cruise missiles and long-range maritime drones. The Moscow-appointed head of Crimea said one arch and a section of roadway had collapsed into the sea. Russia's Transport Ministry suspended both road and rail traffic on the bridge within hours of the strike.

The Kerch Bridge had been attacked twice before, in October 2022 and in July 2023, with partial spans rebuilt after each strike. Tuesday's attack hit a section that had been reconstructed following the 2023 damage, complicating Russia's ability to supply forces in southern Ukraine through Crimea. Russian military bloggers described the strike as the most damaging hit on the bridge since the war began. Rebuilding a collapsed arch is a months-long undertaking, and Russian logistics to Crimea now depend almost entirely on the land corridor through Zaporizhzhia Oblast.

Casualties and the broader overnight campaign

The overnight strikes were not limited to Kyiv. Russian missiles and drones also hit Kharkiv, Dnipro, and Zaporizhzhia in a coordinated wave. Ukrainian emergency services reported at least 14 people killed and 78 injured across all cities combined. In Kharkiv, a strike hit a residential building in the Saltivka district. Dnipro reported damage to a thermal power substation. Zaporizhzhia recorded four civilian casualties from a strike on a market district.

President Volodymyr Zelensky called the bridge strikes deliberate and systematic, aimed at cutting civilian supply lines into Kyiv ahead of winter. He called on Western allies to lift remaining restrictions on Ukrainian use of long-range weapons against targets inside Russia. Western governments have so far given partial approvals for strikes on Russian territory near the border but stopped short of open authorisation. Zelensky's office said the overnight campaign showed Russia was escalating with no regard for civilian infrastructure.
