Trump in Manila for ASEAN. Shenzhen trilateral set for Thursday.

Manila and the ASEAN summit

President Trump arrived in Manila on Tuesday for the U.S.-ASEAN summit, the first stop of a two-city Asia trip. At the summit, Trump held bilateral meetings with the leaders of the Philippines, Vietnam, Indonesia, and Thailand. Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. raised the question of U.S. commitments under the mutual defense treaty after recent Chinese coast guard actions near the Spratly Islands. Trump offered general reassurances without giving specific commitments, according to officials familiar with the discussions.

The ASEAN communique, released Tuesday evening, included language on South China Sea freedom of navigation and called for restraint in the Taiwan Strait. It did not name China by name, consistent with ASEAN practice. Trump signed a bilateral trade framework with the Philippines and announced an expanded military cooperation agreement. No formal deliverables were announced with the other ASEAN members, though several bilateral meetings are scheduled for Wednesday morning before Trump flies to Shenzhen.

The Shenzhen trilateral

Trump will fly to Shenzhen on Wednesday to attend APEC, where a first-ever trilateral meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin is scheduled for Thursday. Trump confirmed that nuclear disarmament and Iran's nuclear program will be on the agenda. Ukraine is not listed as a formal agenda item. A senior administration official said Ukraine's future could come up in informal discussion but that the meeting was designed around arms control and regional security, not the European war.

The meeting is a significant diplomatic departure. No U.S. president has held a joint formal meeting with both Beijing and Moscow since the Cold War ended, and the format implicitly confers a degree of parity between Russia and China on the world stage. The European Union expressed concern that the format could legitimise Russia's presence in Ukraine. Japan and South Korea asked for immediate readouts from the U.S. side after the trilateral concludes.

Ukraine's reaction and the Berlin parallel track

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said Tuesday that he is "deeply concerned" that decisions about Ukraine's future could be made in Shenzhen without Kyiv present. He asked that any discussion of Ukrainian territory or ceasefire conditions be deferred to a format that includes Ukraine and its European allies. Zelensky spoke by phone with French President Macron and German Chancellor Merz to coordinate a joint response if the trilateral produces territorial proposals.

U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are scheduled to meet Ukrainian and European officials in Berlin on Thursday, running in parallel with the Shenzhen talks. The Berlin track is intended to work on security guarantees, postwar reconstruction financing, and NATO-adjacent arrangements. European officials said the two tracks could either reinforce each other or move in conflicting directions, depending on what emerges from Shenzhen.
