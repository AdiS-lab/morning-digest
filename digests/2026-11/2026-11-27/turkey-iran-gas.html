Turkey Extends Iran Gas Deal to 2032. Pipeline Exports Bypass U.S. Naval Blockade.

November 27, 2026

The deal and its timing

Turkey's energy ministry confirmed Friday that it had extended its natural gas import agreement with Iran through 2032, in a deal that keeps Iranian pipeline exports flowing despite the U.S. naval blockade of Iran's Gulf ports. Iran supplies roughly 20 percent of Turkey's natural gas via the Tabriz-Ankara pipeline, which runs from northwestern Iran through Turkish territory to the capital. The original agreement was set to expire in 2026 and had been under negotiation since late 2024. Turkey's energy minister said the deal was a matter of national energy security and that Ankara had the right to manage its own supply contracts.

The U.S. State Department said it was reviewing the agreement and that all countries should avoid commercial arrangements that sustained Iran's ability to fund military operations. The State Department said it would assess whether the deal required any new sanctions waivers or whether it fell under existing exemptions. Turkey's foreign ministry issued a statement saying the extension was consistent with international law and that Turkey had honored its NATO obligations while also acting in its national interest. The deal was signed before the Hormuz closure began but its confirmation Friday made it immediately politically sensitive.

Why pipelines escape the blockade

The extension is strategically significant because Iranian gas flowing through overland pipelines is not subject to the naval enforcement operations that have restricted Tehran's oil shipments from Gulf terminals. The U.S. Navy's enforcement authority under international maritime law extends to vessels on the high seas and in territorial waters, not to fixed infrastructure on land. The Tabriz-Ankara pipeline carries gas directly from Iranian fields to Turkish customers without passing through any maritime chokepoint. Pipeline revenue has become an increasingly important source of income for Iran as the blockade has cut seaborne oil exports to near zero since November 22.

Iran's pre-blockade seaborne oil exports were estimated at roughly 1.5 million barrels per day, generating several billion dollars in monthly revenue at pre-crisis prices. That income has been almost entirely cut off since CENTCOM began enforcement operations. The Tabriz-Ankara pipeline, by contrast, moves approximately 10 billion cubic meters of gas per year to Turkey under a fixed-price formula, generating an estimated $1.5-2 billion in annual revenue for Iran. While far smaller than oil income, pipeline gas has become one of the few remaining hard currency sources available to Tehran during the blockade.

NATO dimension and broader implications

Turkey is a NATO member with the alliance's second-largest military. Its independent stance on both Russia and Iran has been a recurring source of friction with Washington and Brussels. Turkey continued to purchase Russian gas through the TurkStream pipeline after Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, resisting U.S. pressure to diversify away from Moscow, and it blocked Sweden and Finland's NATO accession bids for more than a year before eventually relenting. The Iran gas extension follows the same pattern. Ankara argues it needs energy security to anchor an economy of 85 million people and that it cannot afford to cut off major suppliers while also absorbing the economic pressure of wars in Ukraine and the Gulf.

The U.S. Treasury confirmed it was examining whether the extended deal required new sanctions waivers under the Iran Sanctions Act and the Countering America's Adversaries Through Sanctions Act. Turkey received a waiver for the existing pipeline deal in prior administrations, but legal analysts said the extension into 2032 might require a fresh determination. Democrats in the Senate called on the administration to revoke any existing waivers and pressure Ankara through NATO channels. The White House said it was reviewing all options and that Turkey remained an important partner whose contributions to NATO defense were valued.
