Russia Fires 155 Drones at Ukraine Overnight. Six Killed. Power Outages Across Six Oblasts.

November 27, 2026

The overnight attack

Russia launched 155 drones against Ukraine during the overnight hours of November 26-27, including 83 jet-powered Gerbera models and an assortment of Shahed-type strike drones and Parodiya decoys. Ukraine's Air Force said 110 were neutralized but that hits were recorded at 27 locations with debris striking four more. Six people were killed including an 86-year-old woman in Izium, and 25 were injured. Apartment blocks and an administrative building in Izium were damaged. A warehouse fire broke out in Bila Tserkva district after debris fell on the structure.

Power supply was disrupted across six oblasts as Russia targeted electrical infrastructure in the Kirovohrad region. The state energy operator said emergency shutdowns were triggered in four distribution areas. Repair crews were dispatched overnight, and partial supply had been restored to most affected areas by Friday morning. Ukraine's Energy Ministry said the attack was part of a continuing Russian strategy to degrade civilian infrastructure ahead of the winter months, with the country entering its third winter of full-scale war.

Ukraine strikes back across the border

Ukraine's Air Force and Ground Forces struck back during the same overnight period, hitting the Efremov Synthetic Rubber Plant in Russia's Tula region, which intelligence officials said produces components used in drone propellants. Additional strikes hit oil and gas processing facilities in the Rostov and Bashkiria regions and an industrial area in Nevinnomyssk in Stavropol Krai. Ukraine's General Staff described the Tula strike as a precision operation targeting dual-use industrial output. Russia's emergency services confirmed a fire at the Tula facility and said one worker was injured.

President Zelensky warned Friday that intelligence indicated Russia was preparing another large-scale attack. He said preparations for a new prisoner exchange were underway following the swap framework agreed in Abu Dhabi on November 12. President Trump told reporters at the White House that Putin was ready to make a deal and that his priority was stopping the loss of life. Trump did not announce a new round of negotiations or a date for further talks. No ceasefire proposal was tabled by either side.

Winter infrastructure pressure

The overnight attack was the latest in a sustained Russian campaign against Ukraine's electrical grid that has intensified since October. Ukraine's national power operator Ukrenergo said the grid had been hit in 14 of the previous 20 nights, with attacks concentrating on high-voltage substations and transformer stations in the central and western regions. Emergency rolling blackouts were still in effect in parts of Kharkiv, Zaporizhzhia, and Dnipropetrovsk oblasts as of Friday morning from prior attacks.

The European Commission said at a briefing that EU member states had dispatched emergency generators and transformer equipment to Ukraine under the Ukrainian Energy Support Fund. Germany and France confirmed new deliveries of air defense missiles under bilateral agreements. Ukraine's power system is operating at roughly 65 percent of pre-war generating capacity according to Ukrenergo's most recent public estimate, with the remaining gap covered by imports from EU neighbors and emergency reserves.
