U.S. Navy Disables Sixth Vessel Defying Gulf Blockade. Iran Denies Kuwait Attack. Oil at $147.

November 27, 2026

Sixth vessel disabled as enforcement widens

U.S. Central Command announced Friday that a Navy warship struck and disabled the Palau-flagged M/V Pacific Archer after the vessel departed Iran's Bandar Abbas port and refused orders to halt. CENTCOM said the Pacific Archer was the sixth commercial ship disabled since enforcement operations began on November 22, the day Iran formally announced its closure of the strait. The crew of the Pacific Archer was described as unharmed and was transferred to a third-party vessel. CENTCOM said 158 other ships had complied with diversion orders and been redirected away from Iranian terminals.

Iran's Foreign Ministry rejected any connection to the Pacific Archer and accused the United States of piracy on the high seas. The vessel had departed Bandar Abbas loaded with what the U.S. described as petroleum products and was refused entry by both Oman and the UAE after CENTCOM issued a vessel advisory. The U.S. Fifth Fleet said enforcement patrols were continuing across the southern Gulf in coordination with partner navies from the United Kingdom, France, and Australia. The U.S. Navy has maintained two carrier strike groups in the region since Iran issued its ultimatum on November 22.

Kuwait attack disputed. Mediation continues without breakthrough.

Iran denied carrying out the drone strike on Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait that killed two Italian contractors on Thursday. Kuwait's defense ministry said its investigation pointed to Iranian-origin drones, and Kuwait summoned the Iranian charge d'affaires. Iran's IRGC stated publicly it had not conducted offensive operations in Kuwait. Western intelligence assessments shared with allied governments, according to two officials who spoke to Reuters, concluded the drones were Iranian-manufactured Shahed variants launched from a site in southern Iraq.

Qatari and Omani mediators continued shuttle diplomacy between Washington and Tehran without a reported breakthrough. Iran's conditions for reopening the strait remain broad. The U.S. counter-proposal, transmitted November 23 through Qatari intermediaries, offered to begin sanctions discussions without meeting Iran's preconditions. Tehran said the offer fell far short of what was required. No meeting between U.S. and Iranian officials was scheduled as of Friday evening.

Energy markets and global reaction

Iran's Houthi allies in Yemen claimed a drone strike on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura export terminal overnight Thursday. Saudi Aramco said operations were unaffected and that air defenses had intercepted incoming threats. Brent crude held near $147 per barrel, flat from Thursday's level. The IEA said importing countries had released roughly 70 million barrels from strategic reserves since the closure began. Japan, South Korea, and India continued to source crude from West African and Norwegian producers at premium prices. European spot LNG prices remained near record highs.

China's foreign ministry renewed its call for restraint and said Beijing opposed any action that disrupted freedom of navigation in international waterways. France and the United Kingdom reiterated calls for de-escalation. The Arab League's emergency session in Cairo adjourned without a formal resolution after Gulf Arab members disagreed on language used to condemn the Kuwait attack. Saudi Arabia recalled its ambassador from Tehran, and the UAE renewed its transit advisory against Iranian-registered shipping in the Gulf.
