Russia fires 30 ballistic missiles at Kyiv overnight. Putin rules out ceasefire talks.

Russian forces launched approximately 30 ballistic missiles at Kyiv in seven overnight waves on Thursday, striking energy infrastructure and several transport facilities. Ukrainian air defenses intercepted a portion of the missiles, but officials said multiple substations and a rail switching station were damaged. At least six people were killed and 21 injured, according to city emergency services.

President Putin, speaking at a joint press event in Moscow, said Russia will not enter ceasefire negotiations following "serious attacks" on the Crimean Bridge and Kyiv's escalation of drone operations against Russian territory. The statement came as European governments pushed for immediate talks following the inconclusive Shenzhen summit.

COP31 misses its deadline. Talks deadlocked over fossil fuel language.

Climate negotiations at COP31 in Antalya ran past the official Friday closing deadline with no deal in sight. The central dispute is over fossil fuels. The latest draft text removed all references to limiting fossil fuel use that had appeared in earlier versions. A bloc of oil-producing nations opposed any mention of a phaseout, while small island states and the European Union demanded stronger language.

The $120 billion adaptation finance package for developing nations also remained unresolved. Negotiators said overtime talks would continue into the weekend. Scientists confirmed at the conference that 2026 is on track to be the first full calendar year with average global temperatures exceeding 1.5 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels.

Senate Republicans suspend $350 billion Iran war defense bill

Senate Republican leaders announced Thursday they are suspending efforts to pass a $350 billion supplemental defense package tied to the Iran war. Supporters said they lacked the votes to advance the bill before Democrats take control of the Senate in January. The package would have funded additional munitions production, expanded naval operations in the Persian Gulf, and provided new financing for base hardening in the region.

Republican defense hawks expressed frustration, saying the decision leaves the military without additional funding during an active conflict. The Iran war has cost the U.S. military more than $48 billion since operations began in late February. Senate Democrats, who will hold a 55-seat majority next year, have not committed to reviving the package.

Pentagon study: Iran war has deepened munitions shortfall, contradicting White House

A congressionally mandated government study released Thursday found that the Iran war has significantly worsened a persistent shortfall of key munitions in the Pentagon's arsenal. The report identified critical gaps in precision-guided munitions, air defense interceptors, and anti-ship missiles. It said current production rates will not replenish stockpiles for at least three years under existing contracts.

The findings directly contradict repeated public statements by senior Trump administration officials, including the defense secretary and national security adviser, who have said there are no munitions shortages affecting operations. The Pentagon did not immediately respond to the report's release. Senate Armed Services Committee Democrats called for a hearing.

South Korea's Supreme Court overturns Han Duck-soo sentence. Case sent back.

South Korea's Supreme Court on Thursday overturned the 15-year prison sentence handed to former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo for his role in former President Yoon Suk Yeol's failed martial law declaration in December 2024. The court found procedural errors in how the lower court evaluated evidence and returned the case to the Seoul High Court for a new hearing. Han remains under separate house arrest conditions pending retrial.

The martial law attempt lasted six hours before the National Assembly voted to lift it. Yoon was convicted separately and is serving a sentence. Han had argued he was not aware of the full scope of the plan in advance. The ruling is likely to extend the legal proceedings by at least a year.
