Iran rejects IAEA demand to halt uranium enrichment

Iran's response to the IAEA

Iran's foreign ministry issued a statement Saturday rejecting a demand by the International Atomic Energy Agency board of governors to suspend uranium enrichment ahead of the planned Trump-Xi-Putin summit in Shenzhen. The ministry called the IAEA's statements politically motivated and accused the agency of acting outside its technical mandate at the behest of Western governments. Iran's atomic energy chief said enrichment activities would continue at their current pace.

The IAEA board, meeting in emergency session in Vienna on Friday, had asked Iran to cap enrichment at 20 percent purity as a confidence-building measure before the November 19 summit. Iran's current enrichment level stands at 60 percent purity, far above the 3 to 5 percent needed for civilian power generation and below the 90 percent threshold for weapons-grade material.

The summit backdrop

The Trump-Xi-Putin meeting in Shenzhen is expected to include discussion of Iran's nuclear program as part of a broader set of strategic topics. U.S. officials have said a verifiable cap on enrichment would help create conditions for renewed diplomacy. Russia and China, both IAEA board members, abstained from Friday's vote rather than supporting the demand, which passed with votes from Western and Gulf states.

Qatar's foreign minister, whose country has served as a back channel between Washington and Tehran, said Iran's rejection was not a final answer and that conversations were ongoing. Iran has not entered formal nuclear talks with the U.S. since the 2015 Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action collapsed. Tehran has previously said it would not negotiate under what it calls coercive pressure.

Stockpile and timeline concerns

The IAEA's most recent quarterly report estimated Iran holds approximately 580 kg of uranium enriched to 60 percent. Experts have said this stockpile, if further enriched to 90 percent, could yield enough fissile material for several nuclear devices within a few months. Iran denies seeking a nuclear weapon and says its program is entirely for civilian and research purposes.

The IAEA has also reported that Iran has not provided satisfactory answers about traces of uranium found at three undeclared sites. Iran has refused IAEA inspectors access to those locations for more than two years. The board's demand was partly intended to demonstrate Western resolve ahead of the Shenzhen summit, where the U.S. hopes to persuade China and Russia to support a stronger inspection regime.
