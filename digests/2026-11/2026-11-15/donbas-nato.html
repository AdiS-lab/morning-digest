Zelensky says U.S. proposed Donbas-for-NATO deal

What was proposed

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said U.S. negotiators raised a new idea after the Abu Dhabi talks concluded on Wednesday: Ukraine would formally withdraw from the Donbas in exchange for immediate NATO membership. Zelensky said this was the first time the American side had explicitly linked territorial concessions to a NATO offer. He described the conversation as preliminary and said no written proposal had been delivered.

Zelensky rejected any arrangement he called a surrender but said he could not dismiss an offer if it were put formally to the Ukrainian people in a referendum. He said any referendum would require security guarantees in place first. He added that his government had not yet given the U.S. a formal reply.

Russia's position and what comes next

Russia has treated Ukrainian NATO membership as a red line since before the 2022 invasion. At the same Abu Dhabi session, Moscow demanded that Finland be expelled from NATO as a precondition for any broader settlement, a demand European governments called non-negotiable. The Kremlin has not commented publicly on the Donbas-for-NATO framing.

The next round of talks has not been scheduled. U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff said both sides needed time to consult their capitals. European governments, which were not represented in Abu Dhabi, said they expected to be included in any framework that affects NATO's boundaries or Ukraine's territorial integrity.

The proposal represents the furthest any American administration has gone toward trading Ukrainian land for alliance membership. Critics inside Ukraine said the idea rewarded Russian aggression. Supporters said a NATO guarantee would deter any future attack better than control of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts alone.

Background

The Donbas, comprising Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, has been partially occupied by Russian-backed forces since 2014. Russia formally annexed both oblasts in September 2022 along with Zaporizhzhia and Kherson, though it does not control all of their territory. Ukraine has never recognized any annexation and its constitution prohibits the government from conceding territory without a national referendum.
