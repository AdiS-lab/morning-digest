SpaceX catches Starship upper stage in first-ever tower catch

What happened on Flight 15

SpaceX's Starship system completed its fifteenth integrated flight test on Friday night, launching from Boca Chica, Texas on November 13 and completing 14 orbits over approximately 22 hours. The upper stage, called Ship, was caught by the mechanical arms of the launch tower at Starbase after re-entering the atmosphere and performing a controlled descent. It is the first time the upper stage has been recovered intact rather than splashing down in the ocean.

The Super Heavy booster, which carries the vehicle to orbit, aborted its own catch attempt and landed in the Gulf of Mexico instead. SpaceX said onboard sensors detected an anomaly during the boostback burn and the vehicle defaulted to the ocean landing protocol automatically. The company said the booster landed safely and was being recovered for inspection.

What the catch means for reusability

SpaceX's business model for Starship depends on rapid full reuse of both stages. Each Starship is estimated to cost roughly $100 million to manufacture but SpaceX has projected operating costs of under $10 million per flight if both stages can be caught and relaunched quickly. The upper stage catch on Flight 15 is the last major technical milestone needed before the company can demonstrate same-day turnaround on a fully reusable flight.

NASA has contracted SpaceX to use Starship as the Human Landing System for Artemis missions to the Moon. The first crewed lunar landing is scheduled for no earlier than 2028. NASA said it was monitoring the Flight 15 results and that the successful Ship catch was a positive step for the program's schedule.

The road to Flight 15

The first three Starship tests in 2023 and early 2024 ended in explosions before reaching orbit. SpaceX made rapid modifications after each failure, introducing a new venting system and replacing the original launch mount with a water-cooled flame deflector. Flight 4 in June 2024 was the first to survive re-entry and reach a controlled splashdown. Flight 5 in October 2024 caught the booster for the first time, drawing widespread attention. Each subsequent flight extended the mission duration and added objectives.

The vehicle that flew Flight 15 is the first to carry a commercial payload. The batch of Starlink satellites was deployed in low Earth orbit during the fourteenth orbit. SpaceX said it plans to increase payload capacity on future flights as it refines the catch-and-relaunch cycle.
