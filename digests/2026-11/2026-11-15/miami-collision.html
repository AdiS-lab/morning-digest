UPS cargo plane and Copa Airlines jet collide at Miami airport

What happened

A UPS Boeing 767 freighter and a Copa Airlines Boeing 737 collided on a taxiway at Miami International Airport at approximately 9:40 p.m. Saturday. Both aircraft were taxiing toward their respective runways when the Copa 737's left wingtip struck the nose of the UPS 767. The FAA said air traffic controllers had issued a clearance instructing the UPS crew to give way to the Copa aircraft but the UPS plane continued moving.

Two UPS crew members were treated at the scene for minor lacerations and released. All 166 passengers and six crew aboard the Copa flight were accounted for and reported uninjured. Both aircraft sustained structural damage and were removed from service pending inspection. Airport operations continued without significant disruption. The collision did not block an active runway.

Investigation and context

The FAA opened a formal investigation and said the National Transportation Safety Board would lead the inquiry. Investigators planned to review cockpit voice recorder data, ATC audio, and taxiway camera footage. The FAA said it would not speculate on cause until the data had been analyzed.

The incident came five days after a nationwide FAA ground stop on November 10 caused by a telecommunications outage that left thousands of flights delayed or canceled. That event prompted calls in Congress for an audit of FAA infrastructure. The Miami collision adds pressure on the agency, which has faced scrutiny over runway incursion incidents at several major airports over the past two years.

Copa Airlines and UPS operations at Miami

Copa Airlines, based in Panama City, operates Miami as one of its main international hubs. The 737 involved was operating a late-night service to Panama City and had been fully boarded when the collision occurred. Passengers were held on the aircraft for about 45 minutes before being bused to the terminal via ground transport. Copa said it was arranging an alternate aircraft for the flight.

UPS's Miami operations handle overnight freight sorting for Latin American routes. The 767 was scheduled to depart for Louisville. UPS said it rerouted the cargo to another aircraft and that no shipment delays were expected.
