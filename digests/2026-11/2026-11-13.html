Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO

Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as president, CEO, and board director, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO. Harvey C. Jones becomes the board's first independent chair. Nvidia reports quarterly earnings in five days.

Huang's departure follows a week of revelations about a personal relationship with a woman U.S. counterintelligence officials believe was recruited by Chinese intelligence. Nvidia stock fell another 2 percent on Friday, closing below $4 trillion in market cap for the first time since July 2025. Congress has summoned Huang to testify.

Iran claims it destroyed U.S. radar in Bahrain. Pentagon denies it.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it destroyed a U.S. early-warning radar system in Bahrain, calling it retaliation for a U.S. strike on a telecommunications tower in southern Iran. U.S. Central Command said it shot down two drones and that there was no damage.

Separately, a tanker carrying liquefied natural gas struck a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz. At least four tankers were attacked on Thursday, following strikes on two supertankers the day before.

Van Hollen challenges Schumer for Senate majority leader

Maryland Sen. Chris Van Hollen announced he will challenge Chuck Schumer for Senate majority leader. Van Hollen said the midterm results demand new leadership. He acknowledged he is the underdog but claimed support from more than 10 senators.

Schumer said he is confident he has the votes. Jeff Merkley and Bernie Sanders backed Van Hollen. The secret-ballot vote is expected in December.

Kushner and Witkoff meet Putin in Moscow. Europe to join future talks.

U.S. envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff met Russian President Putin at the Kremlin on Thursday. Both sides agreed to include European countries in future negotiations, a step long sought by Kyiv and European leaders.

Putin rejected the idea of foreign peacekeeping troops in Ukraine, calling it a continuation of war. Witkoff and Kushner flew to Dublin afterward to brief Zelensky.

Microsoft outage disrupts services worldwide

A major Microsoft outage knocked out Outlook, Teams, Copilot, and SharePoint for millions of users across 74 countries. The outage began Thursday afternoon and lingered into a second day on Friday. Microsoft said it was not a cyberattack.

Schools, government agencies, and businesses scrambled for workarounds. Analysts estimated the cost could exceed $7 billion.
