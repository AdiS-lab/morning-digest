COP31 ends with a weak compromise. Gulf states and Russia reject the final text.

Climate negotiations in Antalya ended Sunday morning after more than three days of overtime with a compromise agreement signed by 174 of 198 parties. The final text removes a hard phase-out commitment for fossil fuels, replacing it with a voluntary transition pathway, and establishes a $90 billion annual climate finance mechanism for developing nations, down from the $120 billion target sought by the African Group and small island states. Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Russia, and Iran formally objected to the finance provisions and declined to adopt the final text.

Scientists and civil society groups called the outcome inadequate. The UN Secretary-General said the agreement was a floor, not a ceiling, and urged nations to strengthen their national targets before COP32. Turkey's foreign minister claimed the deal as a diplomatic success for the host country. The EU's climate envoy Wopke Hoekstra said the agreement, while imperfect, kept international climate cooperation alive at a moment of acute geopolitical strain.

NATO holds emergency call after Poland missile incident. Rutte lands in Warsaw.

NATO foreign ministers held an emergency video conference Sunday morning, their second in 48 hours, following Friday's incident in which two Russian cruise missiles and a drone came down in Polish territory. Secretary-General Mark Rutte flew to Warsaw and met with Prime Minister Donald Tusk and President Andrzej Duda, calling the incursions unacceptable and demanding a full explanation from Moscow. The alliance stopped short of invoking Article 5, with Rutte saying consultations were ongoing under Article 4.

Poland's government said radar tracks showed the objects were not strays but had altered course after crossing the Ukrainian border. Germany and France backed a joint investigation and called for a UN Security Council session. The United States sent an additional Patriot battery to Poland's eastern flank. Russia's Defense Ministry denied any missile left its flight path and said the incident was a provocation staged by Ukraine. Ukraine denied any involvement and offered to share its own radar data.

Iran announces 72-hour ultimatum to close the Strait of Hormuz. Oil spikes above $140.

Iran's Supreme National Security Council announced Sunday that it would close the Strait of Hormuz to all commercial and military traffic within 72 hours unless the United States halted all air and naval operations in the Persian Gulf. The statement cited the deaths of 34 IRGC personnel in U.S. strikes over the past two weeks. Within hours of the announcement, Brent crude surged to $141 per barrel, its highest price since the Iran war began. Lloyd's of London suspended new marine insurance for Hormuz transits effective immediately.

The U.S. Fifth Fleet said it would not alter operations and that any attempt to close the strait would be met with an immediate military response. The Pentagon moved an additional carrier strike group, the USS George Washington, into the Arabian Sea. Qatar, Oman, and UAE issued statements urging de-escalation. The EU convened an emergency energy security meeting in Brussels and activated its gas storage coordination mechanism.

Ukraine strikes Belgorod in retaliation. Russia orders partial reserve call-up.

Ukraine launched a wave of drones and long-range missiles at Russia's Belgorod region on Sunday, targeting fuel depots and a railway marshalling yard used to supply Russian forces in the Kharkiv sector. Ukrainian officials said the strikes were a direct response to Friday's mass barrage, which hit three Kyiv bridges and killed at least eight civilians. At least two large fires were burning in Belgorod city by midday. Russia said its air defenses intercepted most of the incoming weapons.

President Putin signed a decree Sunday activating a partial call-up of reserve forces in nine oblasts bordering Ukraine, the first such order since October 2022. The Kremlin said it was a precautionary step in response to Ukrainian aggression and NATO military exercises near Russia's borders. Ukraine's military chief said the call-up would take at least eight weeks to produce combat-ready units and would not affect the front lines before spring. Western analysts said the move appeared designed as a signal rather than an operational measure.

Ethiopia and Eritrea exchange artillery fire along their border. First clashes since 2000.

Ethiopian and Eritrean forces exchanged artillery fire at two points along their shared border in the Tigray region on Saturday night, according to officials on both sides. Ethiopia's government blamed Eritrea for opening fire first at the Zalambessa crossing, the same location where the 1998 Eritrea-Ethiopia war began. Eritrea's information minister said Ethiopian forces had advanced into a buffer zone and been repelled. Neither side reported casualties, but the UN's Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs said aid convoys to northern Tigray had been suspended as a precaution.

The flare-up follows weeks of troop movements on both sides of the border first reported on November 18. The African Union's Peace and Security Council announced it would convene in emergency session on Monday. The United States and European Union issued joint calls for immediate restraint and direct dialogue. Relations between Addis Ababa and Asmara deteriorated sharply after the 2022 Tigray peace deal, which Eritrea refused to sign.
