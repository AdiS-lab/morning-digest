Iran attacks Bahrain air base for second time in three days

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps launched six drones at radar positions at Bahrain's Riffa Air Base early Sunday. It is the second strike on Bahrain in three days. Jordan, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, and Qatar all condemned the attack.

The U.S. announced the deployment of a third aircraft carrier, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, to the region, bringing the American carrier presence in the Middle East to three. U.S. officials separately estimated Iran still holds 1,500 to 2,500 missiles in reserve. They described the estimate as low confidence.

Trump departs for Asia. Trilateral with Xi and Putin set for Wednesday.

President Trump left Washington on Sunday for a two-stop Asia trip. He will attend the ASEAN summit in Manila before flying to Shenzhen, China, for the APEC leaders' meeting. The first-ever trilateral summit with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin is scheduled for Wednesday, November 19. Trump said nuclear disarmament will be on the table.

Trump raised the possibility of meeting North Korean leader Kim Jong Un on the sidelines, saying it would depend on President Xi. South Korean analysts said a Kim visit appears unlikely given no advance preparations were visible.

Ukraine launches largest drone attack on Russia since the war began

Ukraine's Defense Ministry said Saturday night's drone assault on Russian territory was the largest it has launched since the start of the war. The ministry did not specify how many drones were used or which targets were struck. Russia has not confirmed the scale of the attack.

The strike follows Ukraine's disclosure last week that U.S. negotiators raised the idea of a Donbas withdrawal in exchange for NATO membership. U.S. envoy Jared Kushner said Sunday he is working to bring European governments into direct talks with Moscow.

House tables Hegseth impeachment articles by seven votes

The House voted 211 to 204 on Sunday to table eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, introduced by outgoing Republican Rep. Thomas Massie of Kentucky. The articles covered Hegseth's conduct in the Iran war, strikes in the Caribbean, an attack on an Iranian school, and the capture of former Venezuelan President Nicolas Maduro. Eleven Democrats missed the vote.

Had four of those absent Democrats voted yes, Hegseth would have been impeached 208 to 207. Republican Sen. Thom Tillis of North Carolina said he would likely vote to remove Hegseth if the matter reached the Senate.

Senate Democrats confirm Schumer as incoming majority leader

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer told reporters Sunday he is confident he will be elected majority leader when Democrats take control in January. Senate Republicans separately confirmed John Thune as their incoming minority leader by acclamation, with no dissenters reported.

The confirmations come as Democrats prepare to assume the majority following the midterm results. The party currently holds 55 seats after the Mississippi race was called last week.
