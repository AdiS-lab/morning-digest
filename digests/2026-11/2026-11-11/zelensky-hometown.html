Russia launches massive drone attack on Zelensky's hometown

November 11, 2026

What happened

Russian forces launched a sustained Shahed drone attack on Kryvyi Rih, the southeastern Ukrainian city where President Volodymyr Zelensky was born and raised. Residential apartment blocks, schools, and two kindergartens were among the structures struck, with fires burning across multiple districts for several hours. Ukrainian air defenses intercepted a portion of the drones but could not prevent widespread damage to civilian infrastructure.

The scale of the attack was notable even by recent standards. Ukrainian emergency services reported dozens injured and confirmed deaths from collapsed structures. Mayor Oleksandr Vilkul called it one of the most devastating single-night attacks on the city since the full-scale invasion began. Zelensky addressed the nation from Kyiv, saying the strikes were intended to terrorize and demoralize, and vowed continued resistance.

Background

The attack on Kryvyi Rih follows a pattern of intensified strikes on civilian infrastructure as Russia attempts to pressure Ukraine ahead of and during diplomatic engagements. Russian forces struck a Ukrainian aid center on November 5, killing 42 people. On November 9, both sides exchanged strikes in the days just before peace talks opened in Abu Dhabi. The choice of Zelensky's birthplace carries obvious symbolic weight, though Ukrainian officials were careful not to overread the targeting.

The Abu Dhabi talks, which began November 10 with U.S. mediation, hit immediate turbulence when Zelensky alleged that Russian military and intelligence officials had presented fabricated battlefield reports during the first session. The drone attack on Kryvyi Rih overnight, while talks were still technically active, reinforced Ukraine's contention that Russia was not negotiating in good faith. The U.S. mediating team did not publicly comment on the overnight strikes.

What happens next

Ukrainian officials indicated the Abu Dhabi talks would continue despite the attack, but Zelensky's negotiating delegation has asked the U.S. team to formally register Russian bad faith as part of the diplomatic record. Western allies are watching the talks closely: any ceasefire framework would require verification mechanisms that the overnight strikes suggest Russia may not honor.

Russia has not claimed responsibility for the specific drone salvo targeting Kryvyi Rih, which is consistent with its usual approach of denying deliberate attacks on civilians while continuing to carry them out. The Ukrainian military confirmed additional drone launches from Russian-held territory were tracked overnight, suggesting further attacks may be planned in the coming days.
