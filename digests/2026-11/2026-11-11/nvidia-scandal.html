Nvidia shares plummet as scandal deepens

November 11, 2026

What happened

Nvidia stock fell more than 5 percent in overnight trading on Monday after CEO Jensen Huang publicly acknowledged a personal relationship with a woman U.S. counterintelligence officials believe was recruited by Chinese intelligence as an asset. Huang did not deny the relationship but said he had no knowledge of her alleged intelligence role and that he had cooperated with investigators. The statement did little to reassure investors, who have been selling the stock steadily since the scandal broke on November 9.

The company's market capitalization has shed roughly $400 billion since the initial revelations, wiping out months of gains tied to Nvidia's dominant position in the AI chip market. The board of directors held an emergency session Monday. It released a brief statement saying Huang retains the board's confidence while the investigation is ongoing, language that markets read as noncommittal and that some analysts interpreted as a prelude to a formal leave of absence.

The scandal

The original revelation, reported by The Wall Street Journal on November 9, described a multi-year romantic relationship between Huang and a Silicon Valley consultant who had, according to U.S. officials, been in contact with officers of China's Ministry of State Security. The officials stopped short of saying classified information was directly passed, but noted that Huang had access to sensitive export control discussions and briefings from the Commerce Department on chip restriction policy.

The counterintelligence implications are significant because Nvidia is at the center of ongoing U.S. efforts to restrict advanced chip exports to China. Huang has participated in private briefings with administration officials about which chips should be classified as restricted and at what thresholds. The question investigators are examining is whether information from those discussions ever reached Chinese intelligence through the relationship, intentionally or otherwise.

What happens next

The FBI and the Commerce Department's Office of Export Enforcement are both reported to be conducting active investigations. Congressional committees have requested Huang's testimony. No criminal charges have been filed, and legal experts note that personal relationships with foreign nationals, even those with intelligence ties, do not automatically constitute a crime without evidence of information transfer.

For Nvidia's business, the more immediate threat is reputational. Several major institutional investors have begun reviewing their positions. The company's relationships with U.S. government clients, including defense contractors and national laboratories that use Nvidia hardware, are under informal review. A formal leave of absence for Huang, or his resignation, would likely trigger further market volatility before any stabilization.
