The 2026 midterms

back to digest · home

Democrats won the Senate and the House. It was not close. Republicans entered with a 53-47 Senate majority. Democrats flipped seven seats and now hold 54-44, with Alaska and Mississippi still undecided.

Senate seat map

Each rectangle is a Senate seat. Dark = Democrat, light = Republican. The seven flips are marked.

The flips

Democrats flipped seats in states that haven't gone blue in decades.

TexasJames Talarico beat Ken Paxton. First Democrat since 1988.
KansasAdam Hamilton. First Democrat in nearly a century.
MaineTroy Jackson ousted Susan Collins after 30 years.
IowaJosh Turek beat Ashley Hinson.
South CarolinaAnnie Andrews beat Darline Graham.
OhioFlipped Democrat.
North CarolinaFlipped Democrat.

Still undecided

  • Mississippi. Too close to call. Democrats haven't won here since 1982.
  • Alaska. Undeclared.

If both break Democrat, the final tally is 56-44.

Why it happened

JD Vance blamed the Iran conflict. "The reason we lost is the war." Voters cited cost of living, healthcare, and data center backlash. The AI infrastructure story played directly into the campaign. Towns promised jobs by data center developers got noise and water shortages instead.

Schumer opened election night: "Against all odds, we Democrats have won the Senate."

What it means

Democrats now have the votes to push legislation on AI regulation, data center permitting, and tech antitrust. The "national data center moratorium" has real institutional backing. This is the context behind the Nvidia selloff.
