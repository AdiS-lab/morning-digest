Trump departs for Asia. Trilateral with Xi and Putin set for Wednesday.

The Asia trip

President Trump departed Washington on Sunday for a two-stop Asia trip that will keep him overseas through Friday. Air Force One flew first to Manila, where Trump will attend the ASEAN leaders' summit on Monday and Tuesday. He is then scheduled to fly to Shenzhen, China, for the APEC economic summit. The trip marks Trump's first visit to China since his second inauguration.

Large protests met Trump's arrival in Manila. Several thousand demonstrators gathered near the summit venue, and Philippine police reported clashes outside a security perimeter. Trump's motorcade took an alternate route to the summit hotel. The White House did not comment on the protests. Southeast Asian governments have expressed concern about U.S. tariff policy and the ongoing conflict in the Middle East, which has disrupted regional shipping through the Strait of Malacca.

The trilateral summit

The first-ever trilateral summit among the United States, China, and Russia is scheduled for Wednesday, November 19, in Shenzhen. Trump said in a brief statement before departing that nuclear disarmament will be on the agenda, calling it "the most important issue of our time." A White House spokesman said the three governments have been coordinating on the meeting since late October through back-channel contacts. No joint communique is planned. Officials said the format will be a working lunch followed by a bilateral session between Trump and Xi.

Trump raised the possibility of a sideline meeting with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, saying it would depend on President Xi's facilitation. South Korean analysts said no advance preparations for a Kim visit were visible and called it unlikely. Iran's nuclear program is also expected to come up. Tehran rejected an IAEA ultimatum last week, and the U.S. has sought Chinese pressure on Iran as part of any broader deal framework.
