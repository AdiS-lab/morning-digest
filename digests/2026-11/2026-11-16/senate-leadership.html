Senate Democrats confirm Schumer as incoming majority leader

Schumer confirmed, challenge resolved

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer told reporters Sunday that he had secured enough votes among incoming Democratic senators to be elected majority leader when Democrats take control in January. The confirmation came three days after Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland formally challenged Schumer for the position, arguing that the caucus needed new leadership to match a confrontational moment. Van Hollen withdrew his challenge Saturday night after a vote count showed Schumer with a clear majority of the 55-member Democratic caucus.

Schumer said at a brief press conference that the caucus is "united, energized, and ready to govern." He outlined three early priorities: a formal review of the Iran war authorization, a vote on a ceasefire framework if one emerges from the Shenzhen summit, and domestic legislation on prescription drug pricing. Van Hollen issued a statement saying he accepted the outcome and would work within the caucus. Several junior senators who backed Van Hollen received committee assignment assurances, according to two Democratic aides.

Republican response

Senate Republicans separately confirmed John Thune of South Dakota as their incoming minority leader by acclamation, with no reported dissenters. Thune said Republicans would use their minority position to slow Democratic legislation and demand hearings on executive branch actions. Sen. Mitch McConnell, who is stepping down from leadership, did not appear at the Republican press conference. Aides said he was traveling.

The new Senate

Democrats now hold 55 seats after the Mississippi race was called last week. That is the largest Senate majority either party has held since 2009. With 55 seats Democrats can pass most legislation with five defections, though they will fall short of the 60 needed to break a filibuster without Republican support.
