House tables Hegseth impeachment articles by seven votes

The vote

The House voted 211 to 204 on Sunday to table eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. Tabling kills the articles without a direct vote on the merits. The motion was introduced by outgoing Republican Rep. Thomas Massie of Kentucky, who said he filed it as a matter of constitutional duty before his term ends in January. Eleven Democrats missed the vote. Had four of those absent Democrats voted against tabling, the motion to table would have failed and the articles would have proceeded to a floor vote, which Hegseth would have lost 208 to 207.

Republican leaders had expected a wider margin and spent Sunday morning working the phones to secure votes. Speaker Mike Johnson said after the vote that the result showed the House "rejected a partisan stunt." Massie said the closeness of the vote proved the articles had merit and expressed regret that the incoming Democratic majority would not inherit the articles, since they will expire at the end of the current Congress.

What the articles charged

The eight articles covered four categories of conduct: Hegseth's management of the Iran war, strikes in the Caribbean, an airstrike on what the articles called an Iranian school, and the detention and transfer of former Venezuelan President Nicolas Maduro to the United States. Democrats argued the Iran war was conducted without a formal declaration of war and that Hegseth exceeded his authority. Republicans said the articles were retaliatory and that the Iran conflict was authorized under existing war powers resolutions.

Sunday's vote breakdown

Senator Thom Tillis of North Carolina said Sunday he would likely vote to remove Hegseth if the matter had reached the Senate. No other Senate Republican made a similar statement. Senate Majority Leader Mitch McConnell declined to comment.
