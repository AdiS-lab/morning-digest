Iran attacks Bahrain air base for second time in three days

The second strike on Riffa

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps launched six drones at radar positions at Bahrain's Riffa Air Base early Sunday. The base hosts the forward headquarters of the U.S. Fifth Fleet. Sunday's attack was the second Iranian strike on Bahrain in three days. It was also the tenth Iranian military action against U.S. or allied assets in the region since November 5.

Bahraini authorities said two of the six drones were intercepted by air defense systems. The remaining four struck radar positions on the base's northern perimeter. No personnel were reported killed. A Bahraini military spokesman said damage to U.S. and Bahraini radar infrastructure was significant and remained under assessment. The IRGC issued a statement calling the strike a response to what it described as ongoing U.S. aggression against Iranian sovereign territory.

Regional and U.S. response

Jordan, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, and Qatar all issued statements condemning the attack within hours. Saudi Arabia's foreign ministry called it a violation of international law and demanded an emergency session of the Arab League. Qatar's condemnation was notable given its ongoing role as an intermediary between Washington and Tehran on a possible nuclear deal.

The United States announced the deployment of a third aircraft carrier, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, to the region. The carrier joins the USS Gerald R. Ford and USS Harry S. Truman already operating in the Persian Gulf and the Arabian Sea. U.S. officials said the deployment order predated Sunday's attack. The Pentagon separately estimated that Iran holds between 1,500 and 2,500 missiles in reserve, though officials described the figure as low confidence. A senior State Department official said diplomatic channels with Tehran remain open through Qatari intermediaries.
