COP31 misses its deadline. Talks deadlocked over fossil fuel language.

The stalled talks

Climate negotiations at COP31 in Antalya, Turkey ran past the official Friday closing deadline with no agreement in place. The central dispute is language on fossil fuels. The latest draft text circulated Thursday removed all references to limiting fossil fuel production that had appeared in earlier drafts. A bloc of oil-producing nations, including Saudi Arabia, Russia, and several Gulf states, opposed any reference to a phaseout or phasedown. Small island states and the European Union demanded language at least as strong as what was agreed at COP28.

The $120 billion adaptation finance package for developing nations also remained unresolved Thursday evening. Developed nations had proposed a package funded through a mix of public grants and leveraged private investment. Many developing countries said the leveraged model was not equivalent to direct grants and refused to accept it as a contribution toward the agreed finance target. Negotiators confirmed overtime talks would extend into the weekend, with a new target closing time set for Sunday evening.

The temperature milestone

Scientists presenting at COP31 confirmed that 2026 is on track to be the first full calendar year in which average global temperatures exceeded 1.5 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels. The World Meteorological Organization released preliminary data showing that the January through October average was 1.53 degrees above baseline. The 1.5-degree threshold was established as the more ambitious target in the 2015 Paris Agreement. Exceeding it for a full calendar year does not void the agreement but is treated by scientists as a significant marker of accelerating warming.

The confirmation added pressure to negotiators but did not break the deadlock. Delegates from several small island states read a joint statement on the floor of the plenary Thursday calling the gap between scientific evidence and negotiating positions "an act of institutional failure." The EU's lead negotiator said the bloc would not accept any final text that weakened the fossil fuel commitments made at previous COPs.
