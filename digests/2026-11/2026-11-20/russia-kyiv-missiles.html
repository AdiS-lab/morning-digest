Russia fires 30 ballistic missiles at Kyiv overnight. Putin rules out ceasefire talks.

The overnight attack

Russian forces launched approximately 30 ballistic missiles at Kyiv in seven waves between midnight and 4 a.m. Thursday. Ukrainian air defenses were active throughout the night. Officials said a portion of the missiles were intercepted, but multiple substations in the northern and eastern districts of the city lost power. A rail switching station near Darnyts'ka was struck and put out of service. City emergency services reported at least six people killed and 21 injured across the attack sequence. Many of the wounded were civilians sheltering in metro stations when debris struck ventilation structures above ground.

The attack was described by Ukrainian military officials as the heaviest single-night bombardment on the capital since March 2026. Russian forces concentrated the final three waves on energy infrastructure, a pattern analysts said was intended to degrade heating capacity ahead of the winter. Repair crews were deployed before dawn. Officials said full power restoration could take several days at some substations.

Putin rules out negotiations

Speaking at a joint press event in Moscow on Thursday afternoon, President Putin said Russia will not enter ceasefire negotiations in the current environment. He cited what he called "serious attacks" on the Crimean Bridge and Ukraine's escalating drone campaign against Russian territory as reasons for the refusal. He did not announce specific retaliation but said Russia reserves the right to respond to any infrastructure strikes in kind.

The statement was issued as European governments, including France, Germany, and Poland, pushed for immediate talks following the inconclusive Shenzhen summit. The European Union's foreign policy chief called Putin's remarks "a deliberate escalation that undermines any path to a negotiated outcome." Ukraine's foreign ministry said the missile attack and the statement together constituted a formal rejection of the ceasefire framework proposed by EU mediators last week.
