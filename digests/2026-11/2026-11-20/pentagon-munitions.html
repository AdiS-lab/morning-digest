Pentagon study: Iran war has deepened munitions shortfall, contradicting White House

What the report found

A congressionally mandated government study released Thursday found that the Iran war has significantly worsened a persistent shortfall in the Pentagon's munitions arsenal. The report, prepared by the Government Accountability Office and commissioned by the Senate Armed Services Committee in April, identified critical gaps in three categories: precision-guided munitions, air defense interceptors, and anti-ship missiles. It said current production rates under existing contracts will not replenish stockpiles for at least three years even with no further combat consumption.

The report estimated that the Iran conflict has consumed approximately 34 percent of the pre-war inventory of air defense interceptors, 29 percent of precision-guided munitions, and 41 percent of anti-ship missiles. Artillery shells were less affected because U.S. forces were not engaged in sustained ground artillery exchanges. The GAO study said the Department of Defense did not have adequate reserve stockpile targets in place at the start of the conflict and that the gap between pre-war planning assumptions and actual consumption rates was larger than any previous modern conflict had produced in the same number of months.

The White House dispute

The findings directly contradict repeated public statements by senior Trump administration officials. The defense secretary said in a press briefing two weeks ago that there were "no munitions shortages affecting operations." The national security adviser made similar statements in a television interview last month. The GAO report, which is classified at the secret level but whose executive summary was released publicly Thursday, used different language. It said shortfalls were "operationally significant" and that the gap between official statements and the documented inventory levels was "difficult to reconcile."

The Pentagon did not immediately respond to the report's release. Senate Armed Services Committee Democrats called for a hearing and said the administration should not have been asserting there were no shortages while the GAO was documenting them. Republican committee members did not issue a public statement. The report comes one day after Senate Republican leaders suspended the $350 billion supplemental defense bill that would have funded accelerated munitions production.
