South Korea's Supreme Court overturns Han Duck-soo sentence. Case sent back.

The Supreme Court ruling

South Korea's Supreme Court on Thursday overturned the 15-year prison sentence handed to former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo for his role in former President Yoon Suk Yeol's failed martial law declaration in December 2024. The court found procedural errors in how the Seoul High Court evaluated key witness testimony and evidence submitted by prosecutors. The ruling returned the case to the Seoul High Court for a new hearing. Han remains under separate house arrest conditions pending the retrial. The overturning of the sentence does not mean an acquittal. It means the lower court must redo the proceeding according to the Supreme Court's instructions on evidence standards.

Legal analysts said the ruling was narrow. It did not challenge the factual finding that Han had participated in preparations for the martial law declaration. It challenged only the standard the lower court applied to determining what Han knew in advance and when he knew it. Han had argued throughout the proceedings that he was not aware of the full scope of the plan before Yoon issued the order and that he attempted to slow the process once he understood it. The prosecution disputed that account. The retrial will re-examine that specific question under a stricter evidentiary framework.

Background on the martial law attempt

Former President Yoon Suk Yeol declared martial law on the night of December 3, 2024, in what he described as a response to the opposition-controlled National Assembly acting against state interests. The declaration lasted six hours. The National Assembly convened an emergency session and voted 191 to 0 to lift the martial law order, which Yoon was constitutionally required to honor. Yoon was impeached by the National Assembly shortly after and subsequently convicted on charges including insurrection and abuse of power. He is currently serving his sentence.

Han Duck-soo, as Prime Minister, was the second-most senior official in the government at the time. Prosecutors argued he had advance knowledge of the plan and took no steps to prevent it. His defense argued he was given only partial information and tried to minimize the scope of what was happening once he understood it. The Seoul High Court convicted him in September 2026 and imposed the 15-year sentence. Thursday's Supreme Court ruling means that sentence is set aside and the case returns to the lower court, which must hold a new hearing and reach a new verdict. The ruling is likely to extend total proceedings by at least a year.
