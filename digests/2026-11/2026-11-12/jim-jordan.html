Jim Jordan elected House Minority Leader

November 12, 2026

What happened

House Republicans elected Jim Jordan of Ohio as their minority leader for the 120th Congress on Wednesday. Jordan defeated Steve Scalise of Louisiana by a vote of 106 to 73 in the Republican conference. Speaker Mike Johnson did not seek the minority leader position, opting instead to return to a standard member role after Republicans lost their House majority in the November 5 midterms.

Jordan is a co-founder of the House Freedom Caucus, the hard-right bloc he helped establish in 2015. He is the first member of that caucus to lead the House Republican conference. Jordan previously served as chairman of the House Judiciary Committee and was a central figure in the oversight investigations of the Biden and Trump administrations.

What the minority leader role means

Republicans now hold 167 seats to Democrats' 253, with roughly 15 races still uncalled. That is the smallest Republican House conference since before the 2010 Tea Party wave. The minority leader's core job is to coordinate Republican opposition, manage the conference's messaging, and position the party for the 2028 midterms.

Jordan has a confrontational style that made him a prominent figure on oversight committees but also a polarizing one. His two failed bids for Speaker in 2023 exposed significant intraparty opposition. The 106-to-73 margin Wednesday suggests a portion of the conference still prefers a more institutionalist approach. Scalise, who leads the Louisiana delegation and has long ties to the party's moderate wing, is expected to remain in conference leadership in some capacity.

What comes next

Jordan will formally take the minority leader post when the 120th Congress is sworn in in January. He is expected to announce his leadership team and committee priorities before the end of November. Democrats' large majority means Republicans have limited procedural leverage. Jordan has signaled he will focus on oversight investigations into the outgoing Trump administration and push back on Democratic spending priorities.

The incoming Democratic Speaker, once chosen, will set the committee ratios and determine how much investigative resources Republicans receive in the minority. Democrats now have a majority large enough to pass legislation without any Republican votes, which shifts the minority's role primarily toward messaging and building a record for 2028.
