Congo Ebola outbreak is second-largest on record

November 12, 2026

What happened

The Democratic Republic of the Congo confirmed Wednesday that its ongoing Ebola outbreak has become the second largest in recorded history, surpassing the 2018 to 2020 DRC outbreak and trailing only the 2014 to 2016 West Africa epidemic. The DRC Ministry of Health and the World Health Organization put the total confirmed and probable case count above 12,000, with the outbreak continuing to spread in several eastern provinces.

The outbreak originated in North Kivu province, an area in eastern DRC that has experienced repeated cycles of armed conflict and displacement. Health authorities have struggled to maintain a consistent vaccination and contact-tracing operation in areas where armed groups restrict access. WHO officials said treatment centers in affected zones are operating at capacity.

Why eastern DRC is so difficult to contain

The DRC has experienced more Ebola outbreaks than any other country. The virus is endemic to the Congo Basin, and North Kivu and neighboring Ituri province have seen multiple overlapping outbreaks since 2018. What makes this outbreak different in scale is the combination of high population density, sustained displacement from ongoing conflict with several armed groups, and a healthcare system that was already under severe strain before the outbreak began.

The rVSV-ZEBOV vaccine, which showed high efficacy in the 2018 to 2020 outbreak, is available and being deployed. However, the ring vaccination strategy that proved effective in 2019 requires stable contact tracing, which is difficult where populations move frequently and distrust of health workers remains high in some communities. WHO has requested additional international personnel and supplies.

What comes next

The DRC government declared a national health emergency on Wednesday. That declaration unlocks additional domestic health ministry resources and formally opens the channel for accelerated international assistance under WHO protocols. The African Union's Africa CDC has begun coordinating a regional response, as the outbreak has shown early signs of spread to border areas of Uganda and Rwanda, though those countries have not confirmed independent chains of transmission.

Scaling the outbreak to second-largest in history does not mean it follows the trajectory of the 2014 to 2016 West Africa epidemic, which spread across three countries and reached urban centers including Freetown and Monrovia. The current outbreak remains concentrated in a defined geographic zone in eastern DRC. Health officials said early containment in Kinshasa, the DRC's capital and largest city, is the most critical priority in the coming weeks.
