Russia and Ukraine agree to prisoner exchange as Abu Dhabi talks end

November 12, 2026

What happened

Two days of U.S.-mediated negotiations in Abu Dhabi concluded Wednesday without a ceasefire agreement. The talks did produce one concrete result: Russia and Ukraine agreed to a large-scale exchange of prisoners of war. The two delegations did not confirm a number of prisoners to be exchanged, but Ukrainian officials described it as the most significant POW agreement since the full-scale invasion began.

Ukraine held firm on territorial integrity throughout both sessions. President Zelensky's negotiators made clear that Ukraine will not legally recognize Russian occupation of any Ukrainian territory as a condition of any ceasefire. The U.S. mediation team, led by envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff, asked Russia to submit a revised security proposal within 72 hours before the talks closed.

The diplomatic context

The Abu Dhabi talks were the first direct face-to-face negotiations between Russian and Ukrainian delegations since the brief Istanbul rounds in 2022. The U.S. pushed hard to secure both sides' attendance. Russia sent a mid-level foreign ministry team and military representatives. Ukraine sent senior foreign ministry officials but not Zelensky himself, consistent with his stated policy of not negotiating directly with Putin until security guarantees are in place.

The talks were complicated from the start. On November 9, both sides exchanged strikes on civilian and energy infrastructure. During the first session on November 10, Ukraine alleged that Russian military delegates presented fabricated battlefield data. Russia did not respond to that allegation publicly. The November 11 drone attack on Kryvyi Rih, Zelensky's birthplace, took place while delegations were still seated in Abu Dhabi, testing the talks' survival.

What comes next

Kushner and Witkoff flew directly from Abu Dhabi to Moscow on Wednesday evening. The purpose was to press Russia for the revised security proposal the U.S. requested at the closing session. Moscow has not publicly acknowledged what parameters such a proposal would include. Russian state media described the Abu Dhabi outcome as a "preliminary framework" without further detail.

After Moscow, Kushner and Witkoff are scheduled to travel to Ireland to meet Zelensky. That meeting is expected to focus on Ukraine's security guarantee requirements and whether a ceasefire line could be established without formal territorial concessions. European allies have been consulted in parallel, though no European governments were present in Abu Dhabi. A follow-up round of talks has not been scheduled.
