Three tankers catch fire in Strait of Hormuz. Bessent moves family to military base.

November 12, 2026

What happened

Three oil tankers caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, the most significant single incident since Iran began restricting passage in late October. Iranian officials said two supertankers carrying a combined 4 million barrels of crude were disabled and would not be able to clear the strait under their own power. A third, smaller vessel sustained fire damage but remained partially operational. The nationality of the tankers' operators was not immediately confirmed.

Iran's government reiterated that the strait will remain closed to vessels not licensed under its new permit system until the United States formally responds to its seven conditions for preliminary peace talks. The U.S. has not publicly acknowledged receiving those conditions. At least 37 ships have been attacked or disabled near Hormuz in November alone, according to maritime tracking data cited by shipping insurers.

The Bessent development

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved his family to a military installation Wednesday. A senior administration official said threats against Bessent had reached an advanced and credible stage, without elaborating on the source. The move follows a pattern: at least three other senior cabinet officials have relocated their families or increased personal security detail since the Iran standoff began. The White House said the relocation was a precautionary measure and that Bessent is continuing to perform his duties normally.

The escalation against individual U.S. officials is a new element in the standoff. Previous Iranian pressure focused on shipping and military assets. Analysts who track Iran's threat posture said the shift to targeting named individuals reflects a deliberate attempt to raise the domestic political cost of the confrontation for the administration.

What comes next

Shipping insurance rates for Hormuz transit have risen sharply since the tanker fires. Several European energy companies have begun rerouting tankers around the Cape of Good Hope, adding roughly three weeks to delivery times and raising costs. The U.S. Fifth Fleet, based in Bahrain, has not yet received authorization to escort commercial vessels through the strait, though that option remains under active review by the Pentagon.

Iran's seven conditions for talks have not been published in full. Portions leaked to regional media include a demand that the U.S. lift all secondary sanctions before negotiations begin and that the U.S. acknowledge Iran's right to civilian nuclear enrichment. The administration has not formally responded. Trump told Vance on Tuesday to shift focus away from Iran, saying the U.S. does not need a deal, though the White House did not elaborate on what that means for active diplomatic channels.
