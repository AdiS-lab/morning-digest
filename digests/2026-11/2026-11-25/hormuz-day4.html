Iran's Hormuz blockade enters Day 4. Two tankers hit mines. Saudi Arabia intercepts drones over Ras Tanura.

November 25, 2026

Two tankers struck, Saudi terminal attacked

Iran's IRGC Navy reported that two oil tankers struck naval mines in the northern sector of the Strait of Hormuz in the early hours of Tuesday. One vessel was set ablaze. Iran said both ships had attempted to pass through routes it declared unauthorized following the blockade announcement on November 22. AIS-visible traffic through the strait ran at roughly nine percent of its pre-crisis daily average, and actual oil flows were at about half their normal volume by Tuesday evening.

Saudi Arabia's defense ministry said its air defense systems shot down four drones approaching Ras Tanura, the kingdom's largest oil export terminal. It was the first reported attack on a Saudi facility since the Hormuz blockade began. Saudi Aramco said operations at Ras Tanura were uninterrupted but that security measures were under review. The incident raised fears that the conflict was spreading beyond the strait itself to Gulf state infrastructure on a wider scale.

The blockade's growing toll

Brent crude held above $140 per barrel for a fourth consecutive trading day. Lloyd's of London war risk premiums for Hormuz transits remained at record levels set on Day 2. Major tanker operators, including several large South Korean and European shipping lines, announced they were suspending all Hormuz transits indefinitely pending security guarantees. Iran's army commander said the strait would not reopen until the United States met Tehran's seven conditions, which include a full halt to U.S. naval operations in the Gulf and the release of approximately $6 billion in frozen Iranian funds. The White House National Security Council estimated the blockade was costing the global economy approximately $7 billion per day.

Diplomacy stalls

Qatar's prime minister held a second round of shuttle talks Monday, meeting separately with Vice President Vance and Iran's foreign minister. The U.S. counter-proposal, reportedly offering a 90-day suspension of the closure in exchange for preliminary nuclear talks, remained on the table. Tehran had not responded publicly by Tuesday evening. The Pentagon said two carrier groups were on station in the Arabian Sea and that minesweeping assets were being prepositioned off the Omani coast. G7 finance ministers were scheduled to hold an emergency call on Wednesday to coordinate responses to the oil price shock. Five countries most dependent on Gulf oil, including Japan, South Korea, and India, issued a joint statement calling for immediate restoration of freedom of navigation.
