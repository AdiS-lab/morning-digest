NATO holds emergency call after Poland missile incident. Rutte lands in Warsaw.

Missiles in Polish territory

Two Russian cruise missiles and a drone came down in Polish territory on Friday evening, landing in a forested area near the town of Hrubieszow, roughly 20 kilometers from the Ukrainian border. Polish radar tracked the objects from the moment they crossed into Ukrainian airspace, and Polish military spokesmen said the tracks showed course alterations after the objects crossed the border, indicating they were not strays from an anti-aircraft engagement. No casualties were reported, but Polish authorities evacuated several villages as a precaution and cordoned off the impact sites for forensic examination.

NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte convened an emergency Article 4 consultation that night, the first of two in 48 hours. Article 4 allows any member to request consultations when it believes its territorial integrity is threatened. Invoking Article 5, the collective defense clause, was discussed but set aside, with Rutte saying the alliance needed to establish facts before escalating its posture. On Sunday morning Rutte flew to Warsaw and met with Prime Minister Donald Tusk and President Andrzej Duda at the Belweder Palace. Rutte called the incursions unacceptable and demanded a full explanation from Moscow. The United States sent an additional Patriot battery to Poland's eastern flank within hours of the Friday incident.

Russia denies, Ukraine denies, Germany and France call for investigation

Russia's Defense Ministry issued a statement saying no Russian missile had deviated from its programmed flight path and characterizing the incident as a provocation staged by Ukraine to draw NATO into direct conflict. Ukraine denied any involvement, with President Zelensky saying Ukraine had no interest in hitting Polish territory and offering to share its own radar data with NATO investigators. Poland's government rejected the Russian explanation, saying its radar data was unambiguous and had been shared with allied partners in real time.

Germany and France issued a joint statement backing a formal joint investigation and calling for an emergency UN Security Council session, which was held Sunday afternoon. Russia vetoed a resolution calling for an independent investigation. China abstained. The United States said its preliminary assessment, based on satellite imagery and signals intelligence, indicated the objects originated from Russian territory but stopped short of formally attributing them to a deliberate act. NATO's formal determination of attribution is expected within 72 hours, according to an alliance official who spoke on background.

Significance for the alliance

The Poland incident is the most serious direct challenge to NATO's eastern flank since the war began. Previous Russian strikes on Ukrainian infrastructure had occasionally caused secondary debris to land near the border, but analysts said the course alterations captured by Polish radar set this event apart. If confirmed as deliberate, the strikes would represent the first time Russia has targeted NATO territory since the alliance's founding in 1949.

Poland's government said it was operating on the assumption that the incident was deliberate until proven otherwise and placed its air defense forces on the highest readiness level. The Baltic states, Romania, and Slovakia issued statements of support for Poland and called on the alliance to accelerate the reinforcement of its eastern flank. France and Germany, who have historically urged caution on escalation, backed the investigation but resisted Polish calls for a immediate and formal declaration that Russia had attacked NATO. The alliance's next formal step, expected Monday, will be a written demand to Russia for a full accounting of the missiles' trajectories.
