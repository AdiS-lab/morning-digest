Ukraine strikes Belgorod in retaliation. Russia orders partial reserve call-up.

Ukraine's response to Friday's mass barrage

Ukraine launched a wave of drones and long-range missiles at Russia's Belgorod region on Sunday, targeting a fuel depot outside Belgorod city and a railway marshalling yard at Valuyki used to supply Russian forces in the Kharkiv sector. Ukrainian officials said the strikes were a direct response to Friday's mass barrage, in which Russia fired more than 400 drones and 110 cruise and ballistic missiles at Ukrainian cities and infrastructure, with two of those missiles and a drone landing in Polish territory. At least two large fires were burning in Belgorod city by midday Sunday. Russia's Defense Ministry said its air defenses intercepted most of the Ukrainian weapons but acknowledged fires at a fuel storage facility.

Ukraine's air force said it launched 62 drones and 14 long-range missiles at Belgorod targets in a coordinated morning strike. The marshalling yard at Valuyki is one of only two rail supply routes Russia uses to move ammunition and fuel to its forces fighting near Kharkiv. Destroying its storage capacity there would force Russia to reroute supplies through longer lines. Ukrainian commanders said secondary explosions at the fuel depot indicated the strike hit active stocks. The Belgorod regional governor declared a state of emergency and ordered shelters opened across the city. No Russian civilian casualties were reported by Sunday evening.

Russia's reserve call-up decree

President Putin signed a decree Sunday activating a partial mobilization of reserve forces in nine oblasts: Belgorod, Kursk, Bryansk, Voronezh, Rostov, Krasnodar, Stavropol, Volgograd, and Astrakhan. The decree covers reservists who have completed prior military service and specifies an initial call-up of 30,000 personnel. The Kremlin statement said the measure was a precautionary response to Ukrainian aggression and NATO military exercises near Russia's borders. It is the first mobilization decree since October 2022, when Putin called up 300,000 men following Ukraine's Kharkiv counteroffensive.

Ukraine's military chief Valery Zaluzhnyi said the call-up would take at least eight weeks to produce combat-ready units and would not affect the front lines before spring. Western analysts agreed the move appeared designed more as a domestic and diplomatic signal than as an operational measure. The 30,000 figure is well below the threshold needed to change the balance on any sector of the front. Russia's standing army in Ukraine currently numbers around 500,000 by NATO estimates, with significant attrition over the past six months. Military bloggers on Russian social media noted that the nine oblasts named are all in the south and near the Ukrainian border, suggesting the call-up is partly about civil defense preparation rather than frontline reinforcement.

Front lines and outlook

The Belgorod strikes and the reserve call-up came on a day when the front lines in Ukraine showed little change. Russian forces continued to press toward Pokrovsk in Donetsk and held positions near Zaporizhzhia. Ukrainian forces in the Kharkiv sector reported no significant changes following the Valuyki strikes, though commanders said the rail disruption, if sustained, would begin to affect Russian logistics within two to three weeks. The Kerch Bridge, struck three times by Ukraine over the past week, remained closed to rail traffic, and Russia said repairs would take at least six weeks.

The simultaneous escalations, the Polish missile incident, the Belgorod strikes, and the reserve call-up, created a complex diplomatic situation for Western capitals. NATO foreign ministers, already in emergency session over Poland, now faced the additional question of whether Ukraine's Belgorod strikes constituted use of Western-supplied weapons against Russian territory, an issue that several alliance members have handled differently. The United Kingdom and United States have permitted such use. France and Germany have not formally lifted restrictions. No government commented publicly on Sunday on the weapons used in the Belgorod strike.
