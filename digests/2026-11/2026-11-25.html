Iran's Hormuz blockade enters Day 4. Two tankers hit mines. Saudi Arabia intercepts drones over Ras Tanura.

Iran's IRGC Navy reported that two oil tankers struck naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz in the early hours of Tuesday, setting one vessel ablaze. Iran said the ships were attempting to pass through unauthorized routes. AIS-visible traffic through the strait was running at roughly nine percent of its pre-war rate, and oil flows were at about half their normal volume. Brent crude held above $140 per barrel as shipping insurers updated risk assessments and several large tanker operators announced they were suspending all Hormuz transits indefinitely.

Saudi Arabia's defense ministry said its air defenses shot down four drones over Ras Tanura, the kingdom's largest oil export terminal. It was the first reported attack on a Saudi facility since the Hormuz crisis began. Saudi Aramco said operations at Ras Tanura were uninterrupted but that security measures were under review. Iran's army commander said the strait would not reopen until the United States met Tehran's seven conditions, which include a full halt to U.S. naval operations in the Gulf and the release of frozen Iranian funds.

Ukraine strikes two Russian oil refineries. Putin vows retaliation despite Trump requests to stop.

Ukrainian long-range drones struck the Syzran and Kuibyshev oil refineries overnight, setting both facilities ablaze. Both plants are in Russia's Samara Oblast, roughly 800 kilometers from Ukraine's border. Ukrainian drone campaigns have struck 14 Russian refineries in the first 23 days of November alone, cutting Russia's daily crude oil processing by an estimated 45 percent. Russian emergency services were still battling fires at both sites hours after the attack.

Russian President Vladimir Putin said at a press conference that Ukraine had continued attacks on energy infrastructure despite explicit requests from U.S. President Trump to refrain. Putin called retaliation "inevitable" but did not specify a timeline or target. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said Putin could end Ukraine's refinery campaign by ending the war. Trump posted on Truth Social that he was "very concerned" about the attacks and had urged de-escalation on both sides.

Russia arrests Murmansk governor on embezzlement charges. First sitting governor detained since invasion.

Russian law enforcement detained Murmansk Oblast Governor Andrei Chibis on Monday as he arrived in Moscow for official meetings. Investigators said Chibis was suspected of embezzling more than a billion rubles earmarked to build an Arctic highway, as well as 39 million rubles from the Murmansk city construction budget. Chibis, 47, became Murmansk governor in 2019 after serving as a deputy minister for housing and construction. He was placed in pretrial custody pending formal charges.

The arrest is the first detention of a sitting Russian governor since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022. Political analysts said the case signals that the Kremlin is tightening discipline over regional officials amid wartime pressure on state finances. Murmansk Oblast hosts the Northern Fleet headquarters at Severomorsk, one of Russia's most strategically sensitive military facilities. The Kremlin declined to comment on whether Putin had authorized the arrest personally.

Dozens feared trapped after gold mine collapses in Indonesia.

A gold mine collapsed in the West Sumbawa district of West Nusa Tenggara province, trapping an estimated 40 to 60 workers underground, local disaster management officials said. Rescue teams were dispatched from the provincial capital but were hampered by the mine's remote location and unstable ground conditions following recent heavy rain. The mine, operated by a local company under a government concession, employed roughly 200 workers at the site. Some workers escaped before the collapse and assisted in identifying those still trapped.

Indonesia's National Disaster Mitigation Agency activated emergency protocols and deployed heavy equipment to begin clearing debris. The national search and rescue agency BASARNAS said it would take at least 24 hours to assess whether survivors could be reached via secondary access tunnels. Local officials said a preliminary safety investigation would begin once rescue operations concluded. Indonesia's mining sector has faced repeated safety scrutiny, with dozens of serious incidents recorded each year at both formal and informal extraction sites.

Trump says a pardon for Jensen Huang is "always an option." Governance critics push back.

President Trump told the New York Post in an interview published Tuesday that pardoning Jensen Huang was "always an option," one day after Trump confirmed he had called into Nvidia's board meeting to push for Huang's reinstatement as CEO. Huang has not been charged with any crime. The question of a pardon relates to potential legal exposure from the espionage investigation that led to his resignation eleven days earlier. Nvidia's stock, which surged 30 percent Monday on news of Huang's return, pared gains slightly after the pardon comment as traders cited uncertainty about the federal investigation.

House Minority Leader Jim Jordan said the pardon comment was "premature" and that the espionage investigation should be allowed to run its course. Senate Majority Leader Chuck Schumer called for a congressional hearing on presidential interference in corporate governance. Corporate governance experts said Trump's boardroom call and pardon suggestion together represented an unprecedented blurring of government and private enterprise. Huang has not spoken publicly since his reinstatement. His attorney said the matter remained before federal investigators and declined further comment.
