Russia and Ukraine trade strikes before Abu Dhabi talks

On Monday, Russia launched 317 drones at Ukraine overnight, including 105 jet-powered drones. The Russian Ministry of Defense said it targeted four electrical substations in the Kyiv, Dnipropetrovsk, Cherkasy, and Vinnytsia regions. The result: 850,000 Ukrainians lost power.

The timing was not subtle. U.S.-brokered trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi are scheduled for Tuesday and Wednesday. Both sides appear to be escalating to strengthen their negotiating position.

Ukraine hit back

Hours after the Russian strikes, Ukrainian forces hit another of Russia's largest oil refineries. Ukraine has made Russian energy infrastructure a regular target since late 2024, aiming to cut into the revenue that funds Russia's war effort. Officials did not name the specific refinery.

The winter problem

Russia's strategy is familiar: destroy power infrastructure before winter to deny civilians heating, light, and running water. Ukraine's grid has been under systematic attack since October 2022. Each time it gets repaired, Russia hits it again.

President Zelenskyy said Monday that the EU agreed to activate an emergency mechanism to speed up funding for grid repairs. He also called for tougher international sanctions targeting Russia's missile production capacity.

What happens next

The Abu Dhabi talks on November 10-11 are the first direct U.S.-brokered negotiation in months. Expectations are low. Both sides went into the meeting having just attacked each other's infrastructure. The talks are trilateral: U.S., Russia, and Ukraine. Whether they produce anything depends on whether either side is willing to make concessions neither has signaled so far.
