Democrats reach 254 seats, still counting

Four days after election night, the count continues. Democrats hold 254 House seats. Republicans hold 162. Nineteen races remain undecided. Democrats need 218 for a majority and passed that threshold on Wednesday.

This is the best House result for Democrats since 1993, the year Bill Clinton took office. The party flipped over 40 seats, ousting at least 30 Republican incumbents.

Beyond the House

The sweep extends past Congress. Democrats will control at least 30 governorships. In 23 states, Democrats now hold the governor's office and both legislative chambers, up from 16 before the election. That kind of state-level dominance gives the party control over redistricting, election administration, and policy.

The Senate also flipped. Mississippi and Alaska remain too close to call, but Democrats have already secured enough seats to take the majority.

What comes next

Congress returns this week for a lame-duck session. The outgoing Republican majority still controls both chambers until January. The immediate business: the annual defense authorization bill and government funding. The incoming Democratic majority is already preparing oversight of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and discussing impeachment proceedings.

Rep. Thomas Massie of Kentucky, an outgoing Republican, has filed articles of impeachment against Hegseth. It is unclear whether the lame-duck House will vote on them or whether the incoming Congress will take it up.
