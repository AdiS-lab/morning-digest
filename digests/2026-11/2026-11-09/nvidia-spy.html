Nvidia spy scandal deepens

Nvidia stock fell as much as 8.8% on Monday before recovering to close down about 4%. At its low point, the drop erased nearly $400 billion in market value. This comes on top of the $450 billion loss on November 5.

Nvidia has lost roughly a quarter of its value in the past week. The November 5 drop was driven by the midterms and regulation fears. Monday's drop is about something different: espionage.

What happened

On Sunday, CEO Jensen Huang sent a memo to employees admitting to an affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based chip company Megaspeed. A classified State Department memo, first reported by Bloomberg, alleges that Alice Huang was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security to target Jensen Huang.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio had been briefed on the intelligence as early as spring 2026. Republican lawmakers are now asking why the administration did not act sooner.

The probe

Nvidia's board announced an independent investigation led by outside legal counsel. The board said it has "no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised," but acknowledged the seriousness of the allegations.

Republican lawmakers called on Huang to testify before Congress on November 18. The hearing will focus on whether China gained access to sensitive chip design or AI model information through the relationship.

Why it matters

Nvidia designs the chips that power most of the world's AI systems. If China's intelligence services had access to Huang's communications, the implications go beyond one company. It touches the core of the U.S.-China technology competition. The stock reaction reflects that uncertainty.
