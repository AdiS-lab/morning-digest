Trump threatens military action over Iran dirty bomb intelligence. White House says no credible threat.

The intelligence and Trump's warning

President Trump on Friday warned Iran it would face consequences the likes of which no country has ever paid if it pursues a reported plot to detonate a dirty bomb or deploy chemical weapons against senior administration officials in Washington. The warning followed intelligence assessments described this week to CNN and other outlets by seven current and former officials, who said the plot had been under active monitoring for several weeks. Iran's Foreign Ministry called the reports a contrived narrative designed to provide cover for further U.S. strikes on Iranian territory.

The same day, the Pentagon confirmed that Iran shot down a U.S. MQ-9 Reaper drone over the Persian Gulf, calling it an unprovoked act. The drone was operating in international airspace, according to U.S. Central Command. The shootdown was the second involving a U.S. drone in the Gulf since the Iran war began and added to pressure on the administration to define the scope of its military response.

The administration's internal contradiction

The White House said it had identified no credible threat to the homeland, and a senior official told Fox News the underlying intelligence was not new. National Security Council spokeswoman Adrienne Watson said the president's public statement was intended as a deterrent message rather than a reflection of a specific ongoing plot. The contradiction between Trump's remarks and the administration's own assessment drew bipartisan criticism on Capitol Hill, with senators from both parties questioning why the president was escalating publicly if the threat had been assessed as not credible.

Senator Mark Warner, the ranking Democrat on the Senate Intelligence Committee, said the intelligence in question had been briefed to members weeks earlier and was treated by analysts as low-confidence. Republican Senator Tom Cotton said any threat from Iran, however preliminary, warranted a firm response. The episode added pressure to an already fractured debate over how the administration is prosecuting the Iran war, coming one day after the Senate suspended a $350 billion defence supplemental tied to the conflict.
