UK and EU sign post-Brexit reset deal in Brussels. Food trade, emissions, youth travel.

Three agreements, a decade in the making

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen signed three agreements in Brussels on Friday, ending what Burnham called a decade of drift in UK-EU relations since the 2016 Brexit vote. The package is the most significant reorientation of the relationship since the Trade and Cooperation Agreement took effect in 2021. Von der Leyen said the EU wanted the closest possible relationship with Britain going forward and that Friday's signing was a foundation, not a ceiling.

The three deals cover separate but interconnected areas. The food safety agreement eliminates most sanitary and phytosanitary checks on agricultural and food exports between Britain and the EU, with full implementation by 2028. This is expected to reduce delays at Dover and other Channel crossings, which have added cost and administrative burden to British food exporters since 2021. The carbon trading link connects the UK Emissions Trading Scheme to its EU counterpart, creating a combined carbon market of roughly 1.8 billion tonnes of annual capacity. The youth mobility programme offers up to 100,000 visas per year for people aged 18 to 31 from either side to live and work across the Channel for periods of up to two years.

Economic impact and political opposition

Burnham said the package would boost the UK economy by approximately 0.5 percent of GDP, equivalent to around 17 billion pounds. The Treasury said the gains would be phased in as each agreement took full effect, with food trade savings the earliest to materialise. Officials from both sides said negotiations on student tuition fees for EU and UK nationals studying in each other's universities were given an additional six months to conclude and were not part of Friday's signing.

Opposition was immediate. Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch called the youth mobility deal a backdoor return to free movement and said it would be repealed if the Conservatives returned to government. Reform UK leader Nigel Farage said the carbon market link surrendered UK regulatory sovereignty to Brussels. Burnham pushed back, noting that the youth mobility programme was capped at 100,000 visas annually and was not the same as the unlimited free movement that ended with Brexit.
