Germany and Qatar sign broad partnership deal. Qatar pledges 15 billion euros and offers to mediate Iran war.

A 360-degree relationship

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz welcomed Qatari Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani to Berlin on Friday and signed a broad bilateral partnership that Merz described as a 360-degree relationship spanning energy, finance, and security. Qatar pledged an additional 15 billion euros in investment in Germany, expanding on an existing economic relationship that both governments valued at more than 60 billion euros. The two sides also signed cooperation agreements on liquefied natural gas supply, hydrogen infrastructure, and financial services regulation.

The LNG component was described as particularly significant for Germany, which shifted rapidly to importing liquefied gas from Qatar and the United States after cutting off Russian pipeline supplies in 2022. Merz said the hydrogen deal would support Germany's industrial transition over the coming decade and that Qatar had committed to supply green hydrogen at scale by 2030. German industry groups, which have been lobbying for secure long-term energy contracts, welcomed the deal.

Qatar's mediation offer on Iran

At a joint press conference following the signing, Emir Tamim said Qatar remained willing to mediate between the United States and Iran to end the conflict in the Persian Gulf and had been in direct contact with both governments in recent weeks. Tamim said the conditions for a negotiated solution existed and that Qatar was prepared to host preliminary talks at any point. He stopped short of providing details on what either side had communicated to Doha or whether any progress had been made.

Germany has pushed consistently for a diplomatic resolution to the Iran conflict, which has disrupted European energy markets significantly and driven European gas prices to multi-year highs. Merz said Qatar's role as a neutral interlocutor was invaluable to European security interests and that Berlin would support any Qatari-led initiative that brought both parties to the table. The two leaders said they had also discussed the situation in Gaza and the broader question of post-war regional stabilisation.
