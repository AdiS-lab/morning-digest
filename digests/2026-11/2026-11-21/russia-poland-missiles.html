Russia fires 400 drones and 110 missiles at Ukraine. Two land in Poland. NATO calls it a grave violation.

The overnight barrage

Russian forces launched one of the largest aerial bombardments of the war on Friday night, firing more than 400 Shahed-type drones and over 110 cruise and ballistic missiles at targets across Ukraine. Among the missiles were 27 Iskander-M ballistic missiles carrying cluster munition warheads aimed at Kyiv's power distribution network. At least eight people were killed in the capital and 31 were injured citywide. Three bridges spanning the Dnipro River sustained damage, including one carrying a major gas pipeline.

Ukrainian air defenses were active throughout the night but were overwhelmed in several sectors by the volume of incoming weapons. By morning, power had been cut to two northern districts of Kyiv and to large sections of Kharkiv and Zaporizhzhia. Ukraine's commander in chief said the attack was designed to knock out heating infrastructure before the first sustained freeze of winter, which forecasters expect within two weeks. Repair crews were deployed before dawn, with officials warning full restoration at some substations could take several days.

Poland, NATO, and the Trump response

Two Kh-101 cruise missiles and what Polish radar operators believe was a jet-powered reconnaissance drone came down inside Polish territory overnight, one of them approximately 50 kilometres from the Ukrainian border near Hrubieszow. Poland scrambled F-16s, activated its Patriot batteries, and convened an emergency cabinet session before 6 a.m. No casualties were reported on Polish soil.

NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte called the incident a grave and unacceptable violation of the alliance's airspace and announced he would fly to Warsaw on Saturday to meet with Polish defence officials. Germany, France, and the Baltic states called for a formal Article 4 consultation. Poland's prime minister said the incident appeared to be a deliberate provocation timed to coincide with an ongoing NATO exercise simulating a post-war security mission in Ukraine.

President Trump told reporters at the White House that the Polish incident looked like a mistake and ruled out invoking Article 5. The statement put the United States at odds with several European governments and drew bipartisan criticism from senators on the Armed Services Committee. Trump did not indicate whether the United States would respond militarily to the broader overnight attack.
