Trump, Xi, and Putin conclude Shenzhen summit with joint statement. No Iran or Ukraine commitments.

Presidents Trump, Xi, and Putin wrapped up the Shenzhen trilateral on Thursday with a four-point joint declaration calling for United Nations reform, stable energy flows, and increased petroleum supply. The statement does not include any specific commitment to end the wars in Iran or Ukraine. U.S. officials said Iran and nuclear arms were discussed in private sessions, but no binding agreements were reached on either.

Chinese state media described the summit as a "historic peace declaration." Critics and Ukrainian officials said the declaration changes nothing on the ground. Trump told reporters the three leaders agreed to meet again "when conditions allow." European leaders monitoring the talks from Berlin said they were relieved the statement contained no language on territorial concessions in Ukraine.

Ukraine strikes Kerch Bridge for third time in four days. Rail traffic halted.

Ukraine struck the Kerch Bridge connecting Russia to Crimea overnight Thursday, the third attack on the span in four days. Around 15 to 16 explosions were reported near Kerch shortly after 1 a.m. local time. Russian authorities said rail traffic across the 19-kilometer bridge was suspended and engineers are assessing structural damage to the rail section. Road traffic remained open.

Russia has not publicly responded with retaliatory strikes as of Thursday morning. Ukraine's military said the campaign aims to cut Russian logistics to Crimea. The Kerch Bridge has been a target of Ukrainian attacks since 2022 and has been repaired multiple times.

Hegseth in Caracas. U.S. and Venezuela sign military partnership.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth made a surprise visit to Venezuela on Wednesday, meeting with acting President Delcy Rodriguez and Defense Minister Gustavo Gonzalez Lopez at the Miraflores Palace. The two sides signed an agreement covering joint training, intelligence sharing, and defense equipment transfers. Hegseth said the deal will focus on counter-narcotics operations and border security. He called Venezuela "a compliant security partner on its way to rebuilding its military alongside the United States."

The agreement restores military cooperation that Hugo Chavez suspended in 2005. The visit follows a U.S. special forces operation in January that removed former President Nicolas Maduro. Al Jazeera reported that some Latin American governments expressed concern about the speed and terms of the new partnership.

ICJ rules Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya

The International Court of Justice issued a judgment Thursday finding that Myanmar's security forces committed genocide against Rohingya Muslims during military operations that began in 2017. The ruling is the court's first formal genocide finding against a state in a case brought by another country. Gambia filed the case in 2019. The court ordered Myanmar to prevent further genocidal acts and to submit regular compliance reports.

Myanmar's military government, which seized power in 2021, rejected the ruling as politically motivated and said it would not comply. The court has no enforcement mechanism and would need a U.N. Security Council referral to compel action, which China has historically blocked.

Denmark approves Arctic security pact with U.S. and Greenland

Denmark's parliament voted 109 to 0 on Thursday to approve a trilateral security agreement with the United States and Greenland covering Arctic and North Atlantic defense. The agreement includes increased U.S. access to Greenlandic air and naval facilities, joint patrol operations, and infrastructure investment. The vote came months after President Trump threatened to seize Greenland by force, a statement that prompted the negotiations.

Greenland's prime minister called the agreement "a partnership, not a surrender." Denmark said the pact does not alter Greenland's constitutional status as an autonomous territory. The U.S. already operates Pituffik Space Base, formerly Thule Air Base, in northwest Greenland under a 1951 defense agreement.
