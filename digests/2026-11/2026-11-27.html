U.S. Navy disables sixth vessel defying Gulf blockade. Iran denies Kuwait attack. Oil at $147.

U.S. Central Command announced Friday that a Navy warship struck and disabled the Palau-flagged M/V Pacific Archer after the vessel departed Iran's Bandar Abbas port and refused orders to halt. CENTCOM said it was the sixth commercial ship disabled since enforcement operations began and that 158 others had complied and been redirected. The U.S. said the Pacific Archer's crew was unharmed and would be transferred to a third-party vessel. Iran's Foreign Ministry denied any connection to the ship and said the action amounted to piracy on the high seas.

Iran also denied carrying out the drone strike that killed two Italian contractors at Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait on Thursday, though Kuwait's defense ministry said its investigation pointed to Iranian-origin drones. Qatari and Omani mediators continued shuttle diplomacy between Washington and Tehran without a reported breakthrough. Iran's Houthi allies in Yemen claimed a drone strike on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura terminal, which Saudi Aramco said did not affect operations. Brent crude remained near $147 per barrel. The U.S. Fifth Fleet said freedom-of-navigation patrols continued in the southern Gulf.

Russia fires 155 drones at Ukraine overnight. Six killed. Power outages across six oblasts. Trump says Putin wants a deal.

Russia launched 155 drones, including 83 jet-powered types, Gerbera models, and Parodiya decoys, against Ukraine in an overnight attack on Friday. Ukraine's Air Force said 110 were neutralized but hits were recorded at 27 locations and debris struck four more. Six people were killed including an 86-year-old woman, and 25 were injured. Apartment blocks and an administrative building in Izium were damaged. Power supply was disrupted across six oblasts as Russia targeted electrical infrastructure in the Kirovohrad region, and a warehouse fire was sparked in Bila Tserkva district.

Ukraine struck back, hitting the Efremov Synthetic Rubber Plant in Russia's Tula region overnight, along with oil and gas facilities in the Rostov and Bashkiria regions and an industrial area in Nevinnomyssk. President Zelensky warned that intelligence indicated Russia was preparing another large-scale attack and said preparations for a new prisoner exchange were underway. President Trump told reporters that Putin was ready to make a deal and that his priority was stopping the loss of life. No new peace talks date was announced.

Jensen Huang set to face Congress. Republicans call for wide probe. Trump defends him and hints at pardon.

Jensen Huang is scheduled to appear before the House Intelligence Committee in a closed session around December 1 and before the Senate Banking Committee in a public hearing after that. The hearings center on whether Nvidia advanced processors reached China through intermediaries including Super Micro Computer and Megaspeed International. Attorney General Blanche said the Justice Department found no evidence of personal wrongdoing by Huang before his reinstatement, drawing on ongoing probes into those two Nvidia customers. Republican Senator Tom Cotton called the Nvidia-Megaspeed dispute the tip of the iceberg and said it was not resolved by Huang's return to office.

House Minority Leader Jeffries said Congress would pursue a wide-ranging investigation into Nvidia and what he called corrupt abuse of power by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick. Democrats noted Trump's financial stake in Nvidia stock and Nvidia's donation to the White House ballroom renovation. Trump defended Huang at a press conference, described him as 100 percent aboveboard, and said he did not think a pardon would be necessary but it was always an option. Senator John Kennedy predicted Huang would plead the Fifth. Nvidia shares were down slightly on the day but held near their post-reinstatement level.

Some Senate Republicans signal doubts about Section 301 tariffs. Democrats see an opening.

Several Republican senators expressed reservations Friday about the administration's proposed Section 301 tariffs on 14 trading partners, one week after the USTR released its findings. Senator Todd Young of Indiana said a rethink was in order, and Representative Michael Guest of Mississippi said there were areas where he might agree with Democrats. The Section 301 investigation proposed rates from 15 percent for Japan and the EU up to 35 percent for Vietnam and placed half of global economic output under potential tariff pressure. China, the EU, India, and Brazil have all formally objected.

Democrats said they were prepared to work across the aisle on targeted amendments to protect American manufacturers without triggering retaliation on agricultural exports. Any reversal would face a steep hurdle in the form of a presidential veto that would require 67 Senate votes to override. The USTR public comment period is still open and no final rule has been issued. The administration said lower rates would apply to countries actively negotiating trade agreements with Washington.

Turkey extends Iran gas deal to 2032. Pipeline exports bypass U.S. naval blockade.

Turkey's energy ministry confirmed Friday that it had extended its natural gas import agreement with Iran through 2032, in a deal that keeps Iranian pipeline exports flowing despite the U.S. naval blockade of Iran's Gulf ports. Iran supplies roughly 20 percent of Turkey's gas via the Tabriz-Ankara pipeline. The U.S. State Department said it was reviewing the agreement and that all countries should avoid commercial arrangements that sustained Iran's ability to fund military operations. Turkey's energy minister said the deal was a matter of national energy security and that Ankara had the right to manage its own supply contracts.

The extension is significant because Iranian gas flowing through pipelines is not subject to the naval enforcement operations that have restricted Tehran's oil shipments from Gulf terminals. Pipeline revenue has become an increasingly important source of income for Iran as the U.S. blockade has cut seaborne oil exports. The U.S. Treasury confirmed it was examining whether the deal required new sanctions waivers. Turkey is a NATO member and has pursued an independent line on both Russia and Iran throughout the conflict.
