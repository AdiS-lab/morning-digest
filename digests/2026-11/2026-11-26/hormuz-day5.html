Iran's Hormuz Blockade Enters Day 5. Kuwait Drone Attack Kills Two Italian Contractors.

November 26, 2026

Kuwait attack and Houthi claim on Ras Tanura

On the fifth consecutive day of Iran's effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz, Iranian drones struck Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait, killing two Italian contractors working for a U.S.-affiliated logistics company and wounding several others. Kuwait's defense ministry confirmed the attack late Wednesday and said it had activated emergency protocols at the base. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps did not immediately claim the Kuwait strike publicly, but Iranian state media described it as a response to continued U.S. naval presence in the Persian Gulf. The U.S. Fifth Fleet in Bahrain said it was conducting freedom-of-navigation patrols in the southern Gulf.

Iran's Houthi allies in Yemen separately claimed responsibility for a drone attack targeting Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura export terminal, one of the world's largest crude oil loading facilities. Saudi Aramco said operations were unaffected and that air defenses had intercepted incoming threats. A second Houthi drone claim targeting Ras Tanura had been issued on November 25. The two successive claims raised alarm among shipping insurers, who classified the Kuwait attack as an escalation that extended the conflict beyond the strait itself. War-risk premiums rose on routes throughout the northern Indian Ocean.

Diplomacy stalled. Oil reaches $147 per barrel.

Iran's army commander reiterated in a televised statement that the strait would remain closed until Washington met Tehran's seven conditions. These include a halt to U.S. naval operations in the Persian Gulf, the release of frozen Iranian assets held in South Korea and Japan, and an end to U.S. arms sales to Gulf Arab states. The U.S. counter-proposal, transmitted through Qatari mediators on November 23, fell short of these demands. Qatari and Omani diplomats were still attempting to narrow the gap, but no meeting between U.S. and Iranian officials had been scheduled by the end of the day.

IMF PortWatch data showed near-zero commercial transits through the strait for the fifth consecutive day. Brent crude climbed to $147 per barrel, the highest since the blockade began. The International Energy Agency said importing countries had released roughly 65 million barrels from strategic reserves since the closure, covering approximately five days of global demand. Japan, South Korea, and India were coordinating purchases from West African and Norwegian producers. European spot LNG prices hit record levels as buyers sought alternatives to Gulf-routed cargoes.

The strait in context

The Arab League convened an emergency session in Cairo. Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Bahrain called the Kuwait strike an attack on civilian infrastructure. Kuwait summoned the Iranian charge d'affaires. France and the United Kingdom reiterated calls for immediate de-escalation and said they were consulting the United States on possible additional naval deployments to the region. China called for restraint on all sides and said it opposed any actions that threatened freedom of navigation, while declining to endorse specific U.S. counter-measures.

The Strait of Hormuz is the world's most important oil chokepoint. Roughly one fifth of global oil supply and a significant share of LNG trade pass through the 33-kilometer-wide navigable channel each day. Alternative bypass routes exist but fall well short of the strait's normal throughput. Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline to Yanbu carries up to 5 million barrels per day, and the UAE's Abu Dhabi Crude Oil Pipeline to Fujairah adds another 1.5 million. Even combined, these bypasses cover only a fraction of the roughly 21 million barrels per day that normally transit Hormuz.
