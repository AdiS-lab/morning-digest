Trump Meets NATO Chief Rutte at Mar-a-Lago on Thanksgiving. Kushner Says Large Prisoner Exchange Is Coming Soon.

November 26, 2026

The Mar-a-Lago meeting

President Trump hosted NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte at Mar-a-Lago on Thanksgiving, the first meeting between the two since Trump met Putin in Shenzhen a week earlier. Rutte told Fox News ahead of the meeting that keeping Ukraine in the fight was essential and that pressure on Putin appeared to be working. Trump and Rutte discussed allied support for Ukraine and options for increasing diplomatic pressure on Moscow. The White House said the two agreed that any ceasefire must include verifiable terms and not reward Russian territorial gains, though no new commitments on arms shipments or financial support were announced.

The meeting took place against a backdrop of intensifying Russian strikes on Ukrainian territory, including the overnight Kharkiv barrage. Rutte said afterward that NATO's Article 5 commitment was absolute and that the alliance was closely monitoring Russian escalatory behavior, including the nuclear satellite warning issued by NATO intelligence earlier in the week. Trump told reporters the conversation was very productive and that he and Rutte were on the same page regarding a diplomatic path forward, though he declined to specify what that path would look like before the new year.

Kushner and the prisoner exchange

U.S. envoy Jared Kushner separately stated that trilateral talks were on track to produce the largest prisoner-of-war exchange of the war before the holiday season. Kushner described Putin as a political strategist but said the United States held more leverage than Moscow acknowledged. He did not say how many prisoners were involved or which parties had agreed to the terms. Ukraine's President Zelensky said in a statement that Ukraine was waiting for Washington to set a date for a new round of peace talks. No specific timeline was announced from the Mar-a-Lago meeting.

The meeting was the latest in a series of diplomatic contacts following the Shenzhen trilateral on November 19, where Trump, Xi, and Putin issued a joint statement on strategic stability without reaching concrete commitments on Ukraine or Iran. Since Shenzhen, Russia has continued large-scale missile and drone attacks on Ukraine, and NATO has held two emergency sessions over Russian missiles landing in Poland. The gap between U.S. calls for verifiable ceasefire terms and Russia's refusal to discuss a ceasefire at all remained the central obstacle to any near-term agreement.
