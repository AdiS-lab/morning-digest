Trump Proposes New Tariffs on 14 Trading Partners Under Section 301. China and EU Push Back.

November 26, 2026

The Section 301 investigation and proposal

The U.S. Trade Representative released findings from a monthslong Section 301 investigation, proposing new tariffs on 14 major trading partners the administration accused of maintaining excess industrial capacity that undercuts American manufacturers. The list includes China, the European Union, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, India, Vietnam, and several Southeast Asian economies. The administration used Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 as the legal basis after the Supreme Court struck down its earlier country-specific tariffs in February, ruling they exceeded presidential authority under Section 232. Section 301 allows tariffs based on unfair trade practices and has survived multiple legal challenges.

Proposed rates range from 15 percent for Japan and the European Union to as high as 35 percent for Vietnam and China if no trade deal is reached within 180 days. The USTR said lower rates would apply to countries that reach or are actively negotiating trade agreements with Washington. A public comment period of 60 days is required before any final rule can take effect. The 14 economies targeted together account for roughly half of global economic output and represent the largest addressable group ever subjected to a single U.S. trade action.

Reactions and next steps

China warned it reserved the right to take necessary countermeasures and said the new tariffs were unacceptable and contrary to WTO rules. The EU trade commissioner said Brussels would respond with proportionate measures if the proposal moved forward and would bring a formal WTO dispute. India and Brazil also issued formal objections. Japan said it was seeking clarification on the scope of the investigation and would pursue negotiations to avoid the higher rate. South Korea's trade ministry called the action deeply concerning and said it would engage immediately with USTR officials. The collective GDP of the 14 targeted economies is roughly $60 trillion.

The proposal has political support among Senate Republicans and several swing-district House Democrats who represent manufacturing constituencies. However, the Business Roundtable and the U.S. Chamber of Commerce issued a joint statement warning that broad tariffs at these levels would raise consumer prices and disrupt supply chains that American companies depend on. The Federal Reserve chair said the central bank would take tariff pass-through costs into account when evaluating monetary policy. The USTR set a public comment hearing for January 15. Any final tariff order is expected no earlier than late February, allowing time for bilateral negotiations to begin.
