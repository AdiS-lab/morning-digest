Russia Fires Ballistic Missiles and 113 Drones at Ukraine Overnight. At Least Three Killed in Kharkiv.

November 26, 2026

The overnight barrage

Russia launched a large overnight barrage against Ukraine using Iskander-M and Zircon ballistic missiles alongside 113 Shahed-type strike drones. Ukrainian air defenses neutralized 87 of the drones, but 20 locations reported direct hits and debris fell at six more. Residential buildings and an educational institution in Kharkiv were struck, killing at least three people and leaving others trapped under rubble. Power supply disruptions were reported in Kryvyi Rih following a separate strike on electrical infrastructure. The Ukrainian State Emergency Service said rescue teams worked through the night to free people from damaged structures in Kharkiv's Kholodnohirsk and Industrialnyi districts.

Ukraine's air force said the ballistic missiles were more difficult to intercept than the drone wave and were aimed at infrastructure targets across the country. The simultaneous use of drones and fast-moving ballistic missiles is a tactic Russia has refined over the past three months to overwhelm Ukrainian air defenses by forcing them to engage multiple threat types at once. Ukraine's defense ministry said air defense units across the country were activated for more than four hours during the attack.

Ukraine strikes back and bodies are exchanged

Ukraine's Defense Forces struck back across the border. Ukraine hit the Subkhankulovo oil pumping station in Russia's Bashkortostan region, a chemical plant producing components for military explosives, and a power station in the Stavropol region. Ukraine's General Staff reported 167 combat engagements on the front lines over the preceding 24 hours, with the highest intensity along the Pokrovsk and Toretsk axes in Donetsk Oblast. Russian ground forces made incremental advances of less than a kilometer near Velyka Novosilka, according to the Institute for the Study of War.

Russia and Ukraine also exchanged the bodies of fallen servicemen, with 250 sets of remains returned to Ukraine and 22 to Russia through International Committee of the Red Cross mediation. The exchange was the third such handover in November. Separately, U.S. envoy Jared Kushner told reporters that trilateral talks were on track to produce the largest prisoner-of-war exchange of the war before the holiday season. No date was specified. Ukraine's Defense Minister said the number of Ukrainian POWs believed to be held in Russian custody had not changed materially since October.
