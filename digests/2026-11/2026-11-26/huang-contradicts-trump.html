Jensen Huang Tells Nvidia Staff the Board Reinstated Him on Its Own. Trump Disputes the Account. Congressional Hearings Set.

November 26, 2026

The all-hands meeting

Jensen Huang addressed Nvidia employees at an all-hands meeting on Tuesday, his first since the board voted unanimously to reinstate him as CEO eleven days after his resignation. Huang apologized to staff and said he was in daily therapy. He told employees that Trump's public claim to have personally arranged his return was completely untrue and that the board had acted on its own judgment. Huang said he had not asked the White House for any intervention and was unaware of any direct contact between Trump and board members before the vote. Trump had told CNBC on November 24 that he had spoken directly and indirectly with the board and that his intervention was very important.

The contradiction was direct and unusually public for a sitting CEO. Huang did not elaborate on what communications, if any, had occurred between board members and White House officials in the days before the November 24 vote. The board has not issued a statement clarifying its deliberations. Trump's spokesperson said the president stood by his account and that Huang's statement was unfortunate. Nvidia's board chair, in a brief statement, said the board's decision was made independently and in the best interests of the company and its shareholders, without directly addressing Trump's characterization.

Congressional scrutiny and what comes next

The contradiction sharpened political scrutiny of the episode. Attorney General Todd Blanche gave evasive answers at a press briefing on whether Huang remained under federal investigation for the espionage matter that led to his resignation. Huang is expected to testify behind closed doors before the House Intelligence Committee around December 1 and publicly before the Senate Banking Committee after that. Senate Majority Leader Schumer renewed calls for hearings. Some Republicans, including Senator Pete Ricketts, said Huang's conduct during the spy scandal did not support reinstatement regardless of who arranged it. Senate Banking Committee Chair Brown said the public testimony would be essential to determine what the government knows about chip transfers to Chinese customers.

Nvidia shares held near their post-reinstatement levels but were down slightly on the day. The company has not commented publicly on the status of the federal investigation or on whether Huang has cooperated with prosecutors. The Justice Department has not filed any charges in connection with the espionage matter. Market analysts said the contradiction between Huang and Trump introduced new governance risk but that Nvidia's underlying business, particularly its AI chip dominance, remained the primary driver of the stock. The two congressional hearings are expected to be the most significant public reckoning with the saga to date.
