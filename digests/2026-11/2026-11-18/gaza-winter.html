WHO demands unobstructed aid access to Gaza for its fourth winter of war

The WHO warning

World Health Organization Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus called Wednesday for "unobstructed" entry of humanitarian supplies into Gaza as the territory enters its fourth consecutive winter of war. Tedros said medical stockpiles are critically low across all of Gaza and that the collapse of health infrastructure risks a mass casualty event from cold, disease, and lack of surgical care. The WHO said fuel for hospital generators is running out and that surgical capacity in northern Gaza has essentially ceased, with the last functioning operating theaters in that area closing in late October after fuel ran out.

Germany separately called on Israel to open more crossings for humanitarian aid before temperatures drop further. Israel's government said it permits aid entry and that any delays are the result of Hamas interference with convoy routes. The United Nations Relief and Works Agency said it has been able to deliver roughly one-third of the aid convoys it had planned for November due to a combination of access restrictions and security incidents along approved routes.

Health infrastructure collapse

The WHO said that of the 36 hospitals that existed in Gaza before October 2023, fewer than a third are partially functional. In northern Gaza the situation is more acute: Kamal Adwan Hospital, the last major hospital in the north, is operating on emergency backup power with no surgical capacity. The WHO said it had not been able to resupply northern Gaza hospitals since late October due to access denials at the Erez crossing and security incidents on internal convoy routes.

Tedros described the trajectory as "irreversible" without immediate intervention. Cold temperatures increase the risk of hypothermia among displaced populations sheltering in damaged structures, and the WHO warned that overcrowding in remaining shelters creates conditions for rapid spread of respiratory illness. UNICEF said child malnutrition rates in northern Gaza have reached levels it described as a "nutrition crisis" under its standard classification thresholds.

International pressure

Germany's foreign ministry statement on Wednesday is the most direct call from a major European government for Israel to expand humanitarian access since the current phase of fighting intensified in October. France and the United Kingdom have made similar calls in recent weeks. The United States has not issued a public statement demanding expanded access since the Biden administration's last such statement in mid-2024. Israel has said it coordinates all aid entry through its Civil Administration and maintains that it facilitates the maximum safe access possible given security conditions in the territory.
