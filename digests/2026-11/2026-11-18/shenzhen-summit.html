Trump lands in Shenzhen. Xi meets him at the airport. Trilateral with Putin is Thursday.

Arrival and the airport reception

President Trump arrived in Shenzhen on Wednesday for the APEC summit, where Chinese President Xi Jinping met him at the airport tarmac in what aides described as an unusually personal diplomatic gesture. The two held informal talks for roughly 40 minutes in a private lounge before attending the opening banquet, where Russian President Vladimir Putin was also present. Xi and Trump did not hold a formal bilateral session Wednesday but agreed to meet on the sidelines of Thursday's trilateral. White House staff said the airport meeting set a positive tone.

Xi opened the APEC summit with a speech calling for free and open trade and cooperation on artificial intelligence governance. Putin used his remarks to argue that Western sanctions on Russia had failed and had accelerated Russia's economic reorientation toward Asia. Neither leader made public comments about the Iran conflict or the Ukraine war during the opening session.

The Thursday trilateral

The formal three-way summit between Trump, Xi, and Putin is scheduled for Thursday morning Shenzhen time. Trump has described the agenda as including nuclear arms stockpiles and the U.S. conflict with Iran. Ukraine is not listed as a formal agenda item, though White House officials said it could come up in the margins. Trump said he hoped to "get something done" on weapons limits, without specifying targets or a framework.

Russia's advance team has focused on framing any arms discussion around strategic parity, insisting that any nuclear deal must account for all nuclear states and not only Russia and the United States. China has said it will participate in talks on AI and technology governance but has resisted a formal role in arms limitation frameworks. U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are holding parallel talks in Berlin with Ukrainian and European officials on security guarantees and postwar reconstruction, keeping Ukraine adjacent to but separate from the Shenzhen agenda.
