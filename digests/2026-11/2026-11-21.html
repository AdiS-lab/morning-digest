Russia fires 400 drones and 110 missiles at Ukraine. Two land in Poland. NATO calls it a grave violation.

Russian forces launched one of the largest overnight strikes of the war on Friday, firing more than 400 drones and over 110 missiles at Ukraine, including 27 ballistic missiles carrying cluster munitions. At least eight people were killed in Kyiv. Three bridges over the Dnipro were damaged. Two Kh-101 cruise missiles and what officials believe was a jet-powered drone came down inside Polish territory, one roughly 50 kilometres from the Ukrainian border.

NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte called the Polish incident a grave violation of its airspace and said he would travel to Warsaw this weekend. Poland's prime minister said it was likely a deliberate provocation timed to coincide with European military exercises for a potential post-war mission in Ukraine. President Trump said at a press conference that it looked like a mistake and that the United States would not invoke Article 5. Ukraine said the barrage was aimed at knocking out its power grid ahead of winter.

Trump threatens military action over Iran dirty bomb intelligence. White House says no credible threat.

President Trump on Friday warned Iran it would pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before if it pursues a reported plot to detonate a dirty bomb or deploy chemical weapons against senior administration officials in Washington. The warning followed intelligence assessments described this week to CNN and other outlets by seven current and former officials. Iran's Foreign Ministry called the reports a contrived narrative designed to justify further strikes on its infrastructure.

The White House said it had identified no credible threat to the homeland, and a senior official told Fox News the underlying intelligence was not new. The contradiction between the president's public statements and the administration's own assessment drew bipartisan criticism on Capitol Hill. Iran also shot down a U.S. MQ-9 drone over the Persian Gulf on Friday, according to the Pentagon, which called it an unprovoked act.

COP31 enters second day of overtime. No deal in sight on fossil fuels or climate finance.

Climate negotiations at COP31 in Antalya pushed into a second day of overtime on Saturday with no agreement in sight. A revised draft text circulated overnight dropped all references to fossil fuels and removed a $120 billion annual adaptation finance target for developing nations. The European Union's climate envoy Wopke Hoekstra said no compromise had been reached on either issue. Small island states called the draft unacceptable and threatened to walk out.

Scientists confirmed at the conference that 2026 is on track to be the first full calendar year with average global temperatures exceeding 1.5 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels. Turkey, the host country, struck several bilateral clean energy deals on the sidelines and is under pressure to broker a text before the weekend ends. Negotiators from nearly 200 countries remain in the building.

UK and EU sign post-Brexit reset deal in Brussels. Food trade, emissions, youth travel.

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen signed three agreements in Brussels on Friday, ending what Burnham called a decade of drift in UK-EU relations. The deals eliminate most food export checks between Britain and the EU by 2028, link the two sides' carbon emissions trading schemes, and establish a youth mobility programme offering up to 100,000 visas a year for people aged 18 to 31 to live and work across the Channel.

Burnham said the package would boost the UK economy by approximately 0.5 percent, the equivalent of 17 billion pounds. Negotiations on student tuition fees for EU and UK nationals studying in each other's universities were given another six months to conclude. Conservative and Reform UK leaders condemned the deal as a surrender of sovereignty. Von der Leyen said the EU wanted the closest possible relationship with Britain going forward.

Germany and Qatar sign broad partnership deal. Qatar pledges 15 billion euros and offers to mediate Iran war.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz welcomed Qatari Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani to Berlin on Friday and signed a broad bilateral partnership that Merz called a 360-degree relationship. Qatar pledged an additional 15 billion euros in investment in Germany, expanding on an existing economic relationship worth more than 60 billion euros. The two governments also announced cooperation agreements on liquefied natural gas supply, hydrogen, and financial services.

At a joint press conference, Emir Tamim said Qatar remained willing to mediate between the United States and Iran to end the conflict in the Persian Gulf, and had been in contact with both governments in recent weeks. Germany has pushed for a diplomatic resolution to the Iran war, which has disrupted European energy markets and driven up oil prices. Merz said Qatar's role as a neutral interlocutor was invaluable to European security.
