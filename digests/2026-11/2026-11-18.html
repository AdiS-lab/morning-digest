Iran strikes U.S. base in Kuwait. U.S. had hit an IRGC missile site a day earlier.

Iran's army claimed drone strikes on Kuwait's Ali Al Salem Air Base early Wednesday, targeting U.S. Patriot air defense systems and missile launch pads on the base. Kuwait said all incoming drones were intercepted and no damage or casualties were reported. Bahrain separately intercepted additional Iranian drone attacks with no reported damage. The strikes appear to be retaliation for a U.S. precision strike on Tuesday that destroyed an IRGC missile launch site on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz.

U.S. Central Command said the Qeshm strike "was in response to IRGC aggression targeting American personnel and assets." The expansion of Iranian attacks to Kuwait marks the first direct strikes there in over two months, following a period in which the U.S. and Iran had paused active combat operations.

Trump lands in Shenzhen. Xi meets him at the airport. Trilateral with Putin is Thursday.

President Trump arrived in Shenzhen on Wednesday for the APEC summit, where Chinese President Xi Jinping met him at the airport in an unusually personal gesture. The two held brief informal talks ahead of the opening banquet, where Russian President Vladimir Putin also attended. The trilateral summit between Trump, Xi, and Putin is scheduled for Thursday. Trump said the agenda includes nuclear arms stockpiles and the U.S. conflict with Iran. Ukraine is not listed as a formal agenda item.

Xi opened the APEC summit with a speech calling for free trade and cooperation on artificial intelligence. Putin used his remarks to argue that Western sanctions have backfired. U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are holding parallel talks in Berlin with Ukrainian and European officials on security guarantees and postwar reconstruction.

Western intelligence: Putin plans to fight through winter

Western intelligence agencies have concluded that Vladimir Putin is not prepared for serious peace negotiations and intends to continue the war in Ukraine at least until the end of winter. Officials say Putin believes his forces can make further gains in eastern Ukraine and that continued drone and missile strikes on Kyiv can erode Ukrainian resistance over the coming months. Finnish President Alexander Stubb said he doubts a peace deal is achievable by year's end.

Ukraine and European diplomats gathered in Berlin on Wednesday to work out security guarantees and next steps ahead of any formal negotiations. The White House has pressed European allies to offer stronger commitments on postwar security arrangements as a condition for continued U.S. engagement in the peace process.

Ethiopia and Eritrea mass troops along border as war fears grow

Ethiopia and Eritrea have moved forces to within striking distance of each other along their shared border, according to the conflict monitors ACLED and International Crisis Group. Artillery exchanges and armed clashes have been reported near Badme since last week, after Ethiopian military operations pushed Tigrayan rebel forces toward the border. Ethiopian drones targeted positions near Badme on November 12 and Eritrean forces returned artillery fire.

Analysts warn that continued Ethiopian operations against Tigrayan rebels near the border risk drawing Eritrea into direct conflict with Addis Ababa. The two countries fought a devastating war from 1998 to 2000 that killed tens of thousands. Diplomats and regional monitors have called on both governments to open communications and pull forces back.

WHO demands unobstructed aid access to Gaza for its fourth winter of war

World Health Organization Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus called for "unobstructed" entry of humanitarian supplies into Gaza as the territory enters its fourth consecutive winter of war. Tedros said medical stockpiles are critically low and that the collapse of health infrastructure risks a mass casualty event from cold and disease. The WHO said fuel for hospital generators is running out and that surgical capacity in northern Gaza has essentially ceased.

Germany separately called on Israel to open more crossings for humanitarian aid before temperatures drop further. The United Nations Relief and Works Agency said it has been able to deliver roughly one-third of the aid convoys it had planned for November due to access restrictions and security incidents.
