FAA grounds all U.S. flights after telecommunications outage

The Federal Aviation Administration issued a nationwide ground stop at 3:54 p.m. Eastern on Friday after a data overload knocked out radar and communications at air route traffic control centers across the country. Some 4,042 flights were in the air when the order came. The stop lasted five hours, the longest since September 11, 2001.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the failure began at terminal radar approach control facilities. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no indication of a cyberattack. The FAA lifted the ground stop at 10:15 p.m. Eastern. Airlines were still working through thousands of cancellations into Saturday morning.

Trump to meet Xi and Putin at APEC. Nuclear arms and Iran on the agenda.

The White House confirmed that President Trump will hold a trilateral meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin on November 19 in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. It will be the first three-way summit between the leaders of the world's three biggest nuclear powers.

Trump said he plans to discuss reducing nuclear stockpiles and the U.S. war with Iran. A senior administration official told Reuters that no agreements are expected, calling the meeting the start of a conversation. Russia's war in Ukraine will not be on the agenda, the official said.

Turkey and Qatar sign mutual defense pact in Istanbul

Turkish President Erdogan and Qatari Emir Sheikh Tamim signed five agreements in Istanbul, including a mutual defense pact, at the 12th meeting of the Turkey-Qatar High Strategic Committee. The other deals covered consumer protection, education, and cooperation on conflict resolution.

The pact comes as the U.S. conflict with Iran has strained security arrangements across the Persian Gulf. Qatar hosts the largest U.S. air base in the Middle East. Turkey has positioned itself as a mediator in both the Iran and Ukraine conflicts.

IS-linked fighters kill 44 civilians in Ebola-hit DR Congo

Islamic State-affiliated fighters killed 44 civilians in North Kivu province of the Democratic Republic of Congo, according to local officials and the UN. The area is at the center of the country's Ebola outbreak, confirmed last month as the second-largest on record.

Aid organizations said health workers have been unable to reach several affected villages because of the violence. The attack raises fears that the security situation will further disrupt the already-strained humanitarian response.

Ukraine says Russia refused to discuss ceasefire at Abu Dhabi talks

Ukrainian President Zelensky said Russia's delegation at the Abu Dhabi talks told Ukrainian negotiators it lacked the authority to discuss a ceasefire. The talks ended Wednesday with only a prisoner exchange deal to show for three days of negotiations. Russian Foreign Minister Lavrov accused European governments of trying to give Ukraine time to rebuild its military.

U.S. envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff briefed European leaders after meeting Putin in Moscow on Thursday. The next round of talks has not been scheduled. Trump said Ukraine may have to accept territorial changes.
