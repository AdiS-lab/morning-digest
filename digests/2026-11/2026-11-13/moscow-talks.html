Kushner and Witkoff meet Putin in Moscow

November 13, 2026

The Moscow meeting

U.S. envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff met Russian President Vladimir Putin at the Kremlin's Senate Palace on Thursday. The meeting followed two days of U.S.-mediated talks between Russian and Ukrainian delegations in Abu Dhabi that ended without a ceasefire. Kushner said afterward that both sides agreed to include European countries in future negotiations. Witkoff told CNN the agreement was "a very big deal."

The inclusion of Europe is something Kyiv and EU leaders have pushed for since talks began. Russia had previously resisted any multilateral format. EU chief Ursula von der Leyen said Europe would "defend its interests and back Ukraine" in the expanded talks.

Putin's red lines

Putin doubled down on his opposition to foreign peacekeeping troops being stationed in Ukraine. He told the U.S. envoys that any such presence would be considered a "continuation of war." This is a key sticking point. Western leaders and Zelensky have said a credible enforcement mechanism is necessary for any ceasefire agreement to hold.

Russia and Ukraine did confirm the prisoner-of-war exchange agreed to in Abu Dhabi. Zelensky called it the largest swap since Russia's full-scale invasion began. Kushner said he and Witkoff would work to finalize humanitarian relief alongside the exchange.

Next steps

Witkoff and Kushner flew to Dublin after the Moscow meeting to brief Zelensky. The next round of talks has not been scheduled, but both the U.S. and EU expect European representatives to participate. Russia has not publicly confirmed it will accept the expanded format. Meanwhile, drone and missile attacks continued overnight, with Russia sending 317 drones at Ukraine. Ukraine has disrupted nine of Russia's 35 major refineries, about 30 percent of capacity.
