Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO

November 13, 2026

The resignation

Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang resigned Thursday as president, chief executive, and board director, effective immediately. He had led the company for 33 years. CFO Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO and retain her finance duties while the board searches for a permanent replacement. Harvey C. Jones was named the board's first independent chair. For the first time since its 1993 founding, Nvidia will face investors without Huang at the helm.

Nvidia reports quarterly earnings next week. Analysts expect the transition to dominate the call. Several institutional investors have begun reviewing their positions in the stock. The company's market capitalization has dropped below $4 trillion for the first time since July 2025, shedding roughly $500 billion since the scandal broke on November 9.

The scandal behind it

The Wall Street Journal reported on November 9 that Huang had a multi-year relationship with a Silicon Valley consultant whom U.S. counterintelligence officials believe was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security. In an email to Nvidia employees on November 8, Huang wrote, "I was unfaithful to my wife," without offering specifics. The counterintelligence implications are significant because Huang participated in private Commerce Department briefings about which AI chips should be restricted from export to China.

The FBI and the Commerce Department's Office of Export Enforcement are both investigating. Investigators are looking into whether a company connected to the suspected spy helped smuggle billions of dollars worth of restricted American chips into China. No criminal charges have been filed. Legal experts note that a personal relationship alone does not constitute a crime without evidence of information transfer.

What happens next

Congress has summoned Huang to testify. President Trump reportedly called Huang multiple times this week to encourage him to fight calls to step down. The board said Kress will lead the company through the earnings report and the ongoing investigation. Several defense contractors and national laboratories that use Nvidia hardware are informally reviewing their relationships with the company.
