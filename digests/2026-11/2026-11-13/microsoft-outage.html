Microsoft outage disrupts services worldwide

November 13, 2026

What went down

A major Microsoft outage began around 16:00 GMT on Thursday when users started reporting that they could not access Outlook. Within an hour, the outage tracker Downdetector had logged reports from 74 countries. The entire Microsoft 365 suite was affected: Outlook, Teams, Copilot, and SharePoint. Almost 130,000 problems were reported by U.S. users alone in the first hour.

Workplaces, government agencies, and schools were disrupted worldwide. The outage lingered into a second day on Friday. By Friday morning in Europe, servers that Microsoft had restored were failing again. Microsoft said the incident was not the result of a cyberattack or any "malicious" activity but has not published a root cause. Analysts estimated the cost could exceed $7 billion.

The fallout

Government agencies in multiple countries reported disrupted communications. Schools that rely on Microsoft Teams for remote instruction sent students home. Hospitals using Outlook for scheduling shifted to phone-based systems. Several Fortune 500 companies activated business continuity plans designed for extended outages.

Microsoft has faced growing scrutiny over the reliability of its cloud services. A Senate subcommittee held hearings in September on federal agencies' dependence on a single cloud provider. This outage is likely to renew those concerns. CNN reported that Microsoft's email service was "approaching normal levels" by late Friday, but full restoration had not been confirmed.

No root cause yet

Microsoft has not explained what caused the failure. The company said only that it was not malicious. Engineers attempted to restore services overnight but some recovered servers failed again on Friday morning. A full post-incident report is expected but no timeline has been given.
