Van Hollen challenges Schumer for Senate majority leader

November 13, 2026

The challenge

Maryland Sen. Chris Van Hollen announced Thursday evening that he will challenge New York Sen. Chuck Schumer for Senate majority leader. Van Hollen made the announcement on MS NOW's "The Briefing with Jen Psaki." He said voters sent a clear message in the midterms and that Democrats need "new leadership" and "change from the status quo."

Van Hollen acknowledged he is the underdog but said he has secured support from more than 10 senators. He criticized Schumer for endorsing candidates in open-seat primaries, saying the caucus leader should stay neutral. Schumer responded that he is "confident" he will be elected majority leader. The secret-ballot vote takes place in December.

The endorsements

Oregon Sen. Jeff Merkley became the first sitting senator to publicly endorse Van Hollen. Vermont Sen. Bernie Sanders, an independent who caucuses with Democrats, also backed the challenge. Michigan Sen.-elect Elissa Slotkin said she does not plan to vote for Schumer. Texas Sen.-elect James Talarico declined to back Schumer until he heard his "vision" for the Senate.

Van Hollen has served in the Senate since 2017 and spent 14 years in the House before that. He is a progressive who has previously said he is considering running for president in 2028. Schumer has led the Senate Democratic caucus since 2017 and has not faced a serious leadership challenge during that time.

What it means

The challenge reflects frustration among progressive Democrats who believe the party's midterm sweep demands bolder leadership. Democrats now hold at least 55 Senate seats after Scott Colom's win in Mississippi. The leadership vote is by secret ballot, which means public endorsements may not match the final count. Schumer allies say he has privately locked down enough commitments to win.
