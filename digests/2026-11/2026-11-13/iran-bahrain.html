Iran claims it destroyed U.S. radar in Bahrain

November 13, 2026

The claim and the denial

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said Friday that its aerospace force destroyed a U.S. early-warning radar system on the island of Bahrain. The IRGC said the strike was retaliation for a U.S. attack on a telecommunications tower in the southern Iranian port city of Sirik. It called the action a "serious warning" and said it was "on full alert to respond without geographical limits."

U.S. Central Command rejected the claim. CENTCOM said American forces shot down two one-way attack drones launched toward Bahrain with no injuries or damage. Neither the Bahraini government nor its military confirmed that any radar facility was hit. Photos posted on social media appeared to show satellite imagery of a damaged radar site, but they could not be independently verified.

Hormuz attacks continue

Separately, the IRGC said a tanker carrying liquefied natural gas struck a naval mine while following an "unauthorized and dangerous route" through the Strait of Hormuz. Officials also claimed four attacks on oil tankers on Thursday, following strikes on two supertankers carrying 4 million barrels the day before. At least 37 ships have been attacked near the strait in November alone.

Iran has said the strait will remain closed until the U.S. lifts its port blockade, eases sanctions, and stops bombing. Secretary of State Marco Rubio called Iran's conditions "outrageous and ridiculous and really not realistic." Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who leads the sanctions campaign dubbed Operation Economic Outcast, said he would not be cowed by Iranian death threats. Trump said Thursday that "we don't need a deal with Iran."

Bahrain and the Fifth Fleet

Bahrain hosts the headquarters of the U.S. Navy's Fifth Fleet, which runs Washington's naval operations across the Persian Gulf. This was the first Iranian claim of an attack on American military assets in a Gulf country since early September. Diplomatic efforts mediated by Qatar and Pakistan are reportedly continuing despite the military escalation.
