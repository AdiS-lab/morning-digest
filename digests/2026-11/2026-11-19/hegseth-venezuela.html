Hegseth in Caracas. U.S. and Venezuela sign military partnership.

The visit and the agreement

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth made a surprise visit to Venezuela on Wednesday, meeting with acting President Delcy Rodriguez and Defense Minister Gustavo Gonzalez Lopez at the Miraflores Palace in Caracas. The two sides signed an agreement covering joint training, intelligence sharing, and defense equipment transfers. Hegseth said the deal will focus on counter-narcotics operations and border security. He called Venezuela "a compliant security partner on its way to rebuilding its military alongside the United States."

The agreement restores military cooperation that Hugo Chavez suspended in 2005 after accusing U.S. personnel of espionage. The visit follows a U.S. special forces operation in January that removed former President Nicolas Maduro. Rodriguez, who had served as Maduro's vice president, was appointed acting president by the military-led transitional council that emerged after Maduro's removal. Al Jazeera reported that some Latin American governments expressed concern about the speed and terms of the new partnership.

Counter-narcotics context

The agreement's stated centerpiece is joint counter-narcotics operations in Venezuelan waters and along the Colombia-Venezuela border corridor. U.S. Southern Command has tracked a sharp rise in cocaine shipments routed through Venezuelan ports and airstrips since 2020. Under Maduro, Venezuela was formally designated a state sponsor of narco-trafficking in 2022. Hegseth said the new partnership would allow U.S. and Venezuelan forces to conduct joint interdiction operations "within weeks."

Regional and domestic reactions

Colombia's President Gustavo Petro said he was "not consulted" before the Caracas visit and called the agreement "a unilateral security imposition on the hemisphere." Brazil's foreign ministry said it was "studying the terms" of the partnership. Argentina and Chile issued no immediate statement. The agreement requires Senate ratification as a treaty only if it is classified as a formal defense pact. The White House said it would be treated as an executive agreement, bypassing Senate review.

At home, several Senate Democrats said the deal was rushed and demanded a full briefing. Republican Senator Marco Rubio, who had pushed for Maduro's removal, called the visit "a smart move that turns a problem country into a partner." Hegseth flew out of Caracas before nightfall on Wednesday and was back in Washington by Thursday morning.
