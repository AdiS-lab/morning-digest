ICJ rules Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya

The ruling

The International Court of Justice issued a judgment Thursday finding that Myanmar's security forces committed genocide against Rohingya Muslims during military operations that began in 2017. The ruling is the ICJ's first formal genocide finding against a state in a case brought by another state. Gambia filed the case in 2019 under the 1948 Genocide Convention. The court ordered Myanmar to prevent further genocidal acts, preserve evidence, and submit regular compliance reports to The Hague. The judgment passed by a vote of 13 to 1, with the dissenting judge citing procedural grounds rather than disagreement on the facts.

Myanmar's military government, which seized power in a coup in February 2021, rejected the ruling as politically motivated and said it would not comply. The court has no enforcement mechanism of its own. Any binding action would require a referral to the U.N. Security Council, where China has historically blocked measures targeting Myanmar. Human rights groups called the ruling "a historic moment of accountability" even without enforcement attached.

The case history and displacement

The 2017 military operations, which Myanmar called a counter-insurgency campaign against Arakan Rohingya Salvation Army fighters, drove more than 700,000 Rohingya into Bangladesh in a matter of weeks. By 2019, over a million Rohingya were registered with UNHCR in Bangladesh, primarily in the Cox's Bazar camps. Gambia, acting on behalf of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, filed the case in November 2019. The ICJ issued emergency provisional measures in January 2020, ordering Myanmar to protect the Rohingya population while the case proceeded.

Enforcement and what comes next

The ICJ's judgment is legally binding on the states party to the case. Myanmar is required to submit its first compliance report within six months. However, the court has no police power. If Myanmar refuses to comply, as it has already indicated it will, the only escalation path is a referral to the U.N. Security Council under Article 94 of the U.N. Charter. China, which maintains close ties with Myanmar's military government, has vetoed all previous Security Council resolutions on Myanmar since the 2021 coup.

Gambia's attorney general called the judgment "a foundation for accountability that future generations will build on." Bangladesh's foreign minister said the ruling added international legal weight to calls for safe and voluntary Rohingya return. No repatriation arrangement is currently active. The 1.2 million Rohingya in Cox's Bazar remain in what UNHCR describes as indefinite temporary shelter.
