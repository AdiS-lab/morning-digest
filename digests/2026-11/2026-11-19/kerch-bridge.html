Ukraine strikes Kerch Bridge for third time in four days. Rail traffic halted.

The November campaign

Ukraine struck the Kerch Bridge overnight Thursday, hitting the rail section with a coordinated drone and missile salvo. Russian authorities reported 15 to 16 explosions near Kerch shortly after 1 a.m. local time. Rail traffic across the 19-kilometer span was suspended immediately. Engineers were dispatched to assess structural damage to the rail deck. Road lanes on the adjacent road bridge remained open to civilian traffic.

The November 19 strike was the third in four days. Ukraine hit the bridge on November 16 and again on November 17, the same period in which Russia bombed both Kyiv river crossings. Ukraine's military said the campaign aims to cut Russian logistics routes into Crimea before the winter supply cycle begins. Russia has not announced retaliatory strikes in direct response to the most recent attack.

Strategic significance

The Kerch Bridge, completed in 2018, is the only fixed land connection between Russia and Crimea. At 19 kilometers it is the longest bridge in Europe. The rail section carries heavy military freight including armored vehicles and ammunition. The road section handles civilian and light military traffic. Russia has repaired the bridge after each previous attack, with the longest closure lasting eleven days after the October 2022 bombing. Western analysts say sustained damage to the rail section forces Russia to reroute supply convoys through longer land corridors in eastern Ukraine, adding days to delivery times.

Russia's response

Russia's transport ministry confirmed rail services were suspended as of 2 a.m. Thursday. The ministry said emergency repair teams were on-site and that an assessment would be released "within 48 hours." The Russian Defense Ministry did not issue a public statement on the attack as of Thursday morning. Russian military bloggers on Telegram described the strike as "coordinated and heavier than the previous two." Ukraine's military said the bridge campaign will continue as long as Russian supply operations through Crimea continue.
