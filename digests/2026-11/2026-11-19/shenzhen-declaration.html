Trump, Xi, and Putin conclude Shenzhen summit with joint statement

What the declaration says

The three leaders signed a four-point document called the Shenzhen Declaration on Strategic Stability after two days of plenary sessions and bilateral sideline meetings at the Shenzhen Bay Convention Center. The document calls for reform of the United Nations Security Council veto structure, stable global energy flows, increased petroleum supply coordination through OPEC and non-OPEC producers, and a freeze on new offensive weapons deployments in space. No timeline or enforcement mechanism accompanies any of the four points.

U.S. officials said Iran's nuclear program and the war in Ukraine were discussed in private sessions on Wednesday evening. Trump told reporters the three leaders had "a very long, very productive conversation." No joint statement or separate communique on Iran or Ukraine was released. The White House said any further details would be released "in due course."

What was left out

Iran's uranium enrichment program, which has reached 84 percent purity according to the IAEA, was not addressed in the declaration. Ukraine did not appear in the text. European diplomats in Brussels said the omission was deliberate. A senior EU official told Reuters that the three powers "agreed not to disagree in writing" on the two most urgent conflicts. Ukrainian President Zelensky said in a statement that "a declaration without Ukraine is not a declaration about peace."

The statement also does not reference the ongoing Iranian strikes on U.S. and allied bases in the Persian Gulf. Iran's foreign ministry welcomed the declaration, saying the country's interests were "understood" at Shenzhen. That claim was not confirmed by any of the three delegations.

Reactions and next steps

Chinese state media called the declaration "the most significant multilateral document since the Cold War." European Union foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said the summit "produced words, not results." British Prime Minister Rishi Sunak said the U.K. would monitor implementation closely. Japan and South Korea said they expected clarification on the space weapons clause, which does not define what qualifies as an offensive deployment.

Trump said the three leaders agreed to meet again "when conditions allow" and suggested a follow-up summit could happen within six months. No date or location was set. The Kremlin confirmed a willingness to continue contact "at the highest level." Xi's office issued no statement on future meetings.
