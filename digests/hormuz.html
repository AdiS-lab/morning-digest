Strait of Hormuz

Two oil tankers and a tugboat caught fire after attempting unauthorized routes through the Strait of Hormuz. One tanker struck a mine. Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps confirmed the incidents.

Why this matters

The Strait of Hormuz is 21 miles wide at its narrowest. Roughly 20% of the world's oil supply passes through it daily. That's about 21 million barrels per day. If the strait is blocked or mined, global oil prices spike immediately.

Who controls it

Iran's coastline forms the northern shore. Oman and the UAE form the southern shore. The shipping lanes run through Omani waters, but Iran has the military capacity to threaten traffic from its side. Iran has mined the strait before, in 1988.

What Iran said

Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei said Iran's control of the strait had shattered "the myth of America's invincibility." This is escalatory language. Whether it reflects actual policy or is rhetorical posturing aimed at domestic audiences is unclear.
